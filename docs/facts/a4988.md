**What it is.** A 0.6 x 0.8 in (15.24 x 20.32 mm) breakout board for one Allegro A4988 chip. The chip contains two full
H-bridges (one per motor coil) made of N-channel DMOS FETs, a translator that turns STEP/DIR pulses into the right coil
current levels (full, 1/2, 1/4, 1/8, 1/16 step), two current DACs, and a fixed-off-time PWM chopper that
regulates each coil's current by watching the voltage across a sense resistor. The carrier adds the two sense resistors,
a trimpot that sets the current limit (VREF), the charge-pump and regulator capacitors, a few pull resistors and 16
0.1 in header holes. It runs a motor from 8-35 V, takes 3-5.5 V logic, and delivers about 1 A per coil without a heat
sink, up to 2 A per coil with enough cooling [1][6].

## 3. Electrical

### 3.1 The chip (A4988) [6]

- No MCU: a fixed-function mixed-signal driver. Two full H-bridges of N-channel DMOS FETs, Rds(on) 320 mΩ typ / 430 mΩ
  max per FET at 1.5 A (source and sink), so one coil path (source + sink) is ~0.64 Ω typ.
- Translator: each low-to-high STEP edge advances one increment of the selected resolution; DIR sets direction.
- Current DAC per bridge: Itrip = (%ITripMAX/100) × ITripMAX, with ITripMAX = VREF / (8 × RS). %ITripMAX steps
  follow a sine table (e.g. 100 %, 70.71 %, 38.27 % ...); full step uses both coils at 70.71 %.
- Chopper: fixed off-time PWM. Off-time from the ROSC pin: tied to VDD or GND → 30 µs internal (20-40 µs); through a
  resistor to GND → tOFF ≈ ROSC/825 µs. **On the Pololu carrier ROSC has R4 = 10 kΩ to GND** [10] → tOFF ≈ 12 µs,
  automatic mixed decay (mixed when current is falling, slow when rising; slow decay in full step). Pololu: "configured
  for mixed decay mode" [1]. Mixed decay = fast decay for the first 31.25 % of tOFF then slow decay.
- Blank time tBLANK ≈ 1 µs (0.7-1.3). Crossover dead time 100-800 ns (475 typ).
- Synchronous rectification (no external Schottky diodes needed).
- Charge pump (CP1/CP2 + VCP) makes a gate drive above VBB for the high-side FETs; VREG ≈ 7 V internal for the low side.
- Thermal resistance RθJA = 32 °C/W on a JEDEC four-layer board (the Pololu carrier is smaller; Pololu: ~1 A per coil
  without a heat sink).

### 3.3 Logic levels and limits

- Inputs: VIH ≥ 0.7 × VDD, VIL ≤ 0.3 × VDD, hysteresis 5-19 % of VDD (11 % typ) [6]. With VDD = 5 V: high ≥ 3.5 V,
  low ≤ 1.5 V. With VDD = 3.3 V: high ≥ 2.31 V. **Logic inputs must stay ≤ 5.5 V and ≤ the abs max; they are not
  tolerant of more than 5.5 V.** Run VDD from the same rail as the MCU (5 V Uno/Nano, 3.3 V ESP32/Pico).
- Input current < 1 µA typ, ±20 µA max. No logic outputs (the A4988 has no fault pin).
- Output current ±2 A absolute max per coil; overcurrent protection threshold IOCPST ≥ 2.1 A [6].
- Continuous per coil: **≈1 A without heat sink or forced air; 2 A with sufficient additional cooling** [1][2].
- Internal pull-downs: MS1 100 kΩ, MS2 50 kΩ, MS3 100 kΩ [6]. Board pulls [10]: SLP 100 kΩ pull-up to VDD (R3),
  EN 100 kΩ pull-down to GND (R6) (so enabled by default), **RST floating** (no pull, tie to SLP), STEP and DIR
  floating (must be driven) [1].

### 3.4 Current limit (VREF)

- Formula [1][6]: **I_MAX = VREF / (8 × R_CS)**, VREF = 8 × I_MAX × R_CS.
- R_CS = **0.068 Ω** on all units since 2017 (white resistors, "R068"); **0.050 Ω** before (green resistors) [1][10].
- With 0.068 Ω: VREF = 0.544 × I_MAX. 1.0 A → **0.544 V** (Pololu rounds to 540 mV). For 1.0 A per coil in full step
  set I_MAX = 1.4 A → VREF ≈ 0.77 V [1]. With 0.050 Ω: VREF = 0.4 × I_MAX (1.0 A → 0.40 V).
- I_MAX is the **vector magnitude** sqrt(I1² + I2²); in full step each coil gets 70.71 % of I_MAX [1][6].
- Where to measure: DMM COM on GND, red probe on **the VREF via at (6.10, 6.98)** (circled on the bottom silk) [1][4],
  or on the trimpot's metal rotor/wiper (the wiper is the VREF node per the schematic [10]; common practice, the
  rotor-is-wiper detail unverified for this exact pot part).
- Turning the pot **clockwise raises VREF** [10]. Set it with no STEP pulses and re-check if VDD changes [1].
- VREF input range 0-4 V; 5.5 V absolute max; trip error ±5 % at 70.71 %/100 % (VREF = 2 V) [6].
- Maximum VREF the pot can reach = VDD × R9 / (R5 + R9); R9's value is not on the schematic (unverified).
- Alternative method: full step, motor holding, ammeter in series with one coil; that reads 70 % of the limit [1].

### 3.5 ADC / PWM / DAC / USB / LEDs / buttons / sensors

- ADC, PWM outputs, USB-serial, debug chip: **none**. The two internal current DACs are not user-accessible.
- **LEDs: none.** The board has no power LED. **Buttons/switches: none.** Sensors: only the two current-sense
  resistors (and the chip's internal thermal sensor).

### 3.6 Protections and states [6]

- ENABLE (active low): high = all FET outputs off (motor free, no holding torque); translator, STEP, DIR and MSx stay
  active and keep counting.
- SLEEP (active low): low = sleep, outputs, regulator and charge pump off, ~10 µA each rail. On waking the chip drives
  the motor to the Home microstep position; **wait 1 ms after SLEEP goes high before the first STEP** (charge pump).
- RESET (active low): sets translator to Home and turns all FETs off; STEP ignored while low.
- Thermal shutdown at **TJ ≈ 165 °C**, 15 °C hysteresis: outputs off until it cools, translator resets to Home.
- UVLO on VDD (2.8 V rising) and on VCP: outputs off, translator Home at power-on.
- Short to ground: latched off until SLEEP is pulsed high or VDD is removed. Shorted load (A to B): repeating off-time
  cycles, protected.
- Crossover-current (shoot-through) protection.
- **No reverse-polarity protection** on either supply [2].

the original and valid: black → 2B, green → 2A, red → 1A, blue → 1B. Swapping the two wires of one coil reverses
direction; mixing wires from different coils makes the motor buzz and not turn. Find pairs with a meter: ~1.65 Ω
between the two wires of a coil, open circuit between coils.

## 5. Programming

- Nothing to program on the carrier. The MCU makes STEP/DIR pulses. Arduino (Uno/Nano) with plain digitalWrite or with
  **AccelStepper** (author's site lists 1.66 [12]; Arduino Library Manager/GitHub mirror 1.64 [12]; which version
  the Library Manager serves in Oct 2026 is unverified). AccelStepper interface for this board:
  `AccelStepper stepper(AccelStepper::DRIVER, STEP_PIN, DIR_PIN);` (DRIVER = 1), then `setMaxSpeed()`,
  `setAcceleration()`, `moveTo()`/`move()`, and `run()` in loop() or blocking `runToPosition()`;
  `setEnablePin()`, `setPinsInverted()`, `setMinPulseWidth()` (µs) exist.
- Upload path is the MCU's (avrdude for a Nano: "avrdude: ... bytes of flash written"). The driver needs no reset,
  has no auto-reset and no factory program. At power-up with VDD and VMOT present and RST/SLP high, it energises the
  coils at the Home position and **holds** (both coils at 70.71 % of I_MAX in full step).

## 6. Real failure modes

| fault | what physically happens | user observes |
|---|---|---|
| Reverse polarity on VMOT | no protection [2]: current flows through the H-bridge FET body diodes (and the bulk cap reverse-biased); with a stiff supply the A4988 is destroyed, an electrolytic can vent | instant failure, possible pop/smoke from the chip, hot chip, supply current-limits; board dead afterwards |
| Reverse polarity on VDD | no protection; exceeds -0.3 V abs max on VDD; ESD/substrate diodes conduct | chip likely damaged, warm/hot; driver unresponsive |
| Over-voltage on VMOT | > 35 V abs max (motor outputs 37 V). Also LC ringing at plug-in with long leads and only ceramic caps can spike above 35 V **even from a 12 V supply** [1] | chip dies (often silently, sometimes smoke), VMOT shorted to GND afterwards |
| Over-voltage on VDD / logic | > 5.5 V abs max | chip damage |
| Brown-out | VDD below 2.8 V UVLO (2.7-2.9) → outputs off, translator back to Home; VCP undervoltage likewise [6]. VMOT sag below 8 V → outside rating, torque collapses | motor releases or jumps to Home, lost position (missed steps), no reset of the MCU necessarily |
| Over-current / short | OCP ≥ 2.1 A: short to ground latches off until SLEEP toggled or VDD cycled; shorted load chops with tOFF period [6]. Set current too high without heat sink → thermal shutdown at 165 °C | motor stops, then restarts when cool (thermal cycling: stutter every few seconds); board too hot to touch "long before the chip overheats" [1] |
| Thermal shutdown | TJ 165 °C, 15 °C hysteresis; outputs off, translator Home | intermittent stop/start, missed steps, hot smell possible |
| Disconnect/connect motor while powered | coil inductance keeps current flowing; opening the circuit forces V = L·di/dt across the open contact/outputs, an inductive spike beyond the 37 V output rating; also arcing at the connector | Pololu: "can destroy the driver" [1]. Often a dead A4988 with no visible sign; sometimes a crack/burn mark on the QFN |
| Cold solder joint | on a header pin: intermittent contact; on a motor pin it is the same as unplugging live (spike); on VMOT/GND intermittent supply → LC spikes | jitter, motor buzz without turning, random resets of position, eventually a dead driver |
| 5 V into a 3.3 V-powered driver's logic pin | logic inputs are rated to 5.5 V absolute regardless of VDD [6], so 5 V on STEP with VDD = 3.3 V is within the absolute max (but above VDD; injection through input ESD structures is not specified) (unverified whether this is safe long-term) | usually works; not recommended |
| Shorting 5 V to GND | the carrier has no 5 V rail; shorting VDD to GND shorts the MCU's 5 V rail (USB polyfuse on an Uno, or the Nano's diode/USB supply sags) | MCU resets/USB disconnects, polyfuse warms; driver unpowered (UVLO) |
| Shorting VMOT to GND | the supply's own limit; a LiPo can deliver tens of amps | wires/traces heat, pads lift, battery puffs |
| Beginner traps | RST left floating (driver never runs or runs erratically); no VMOT (logic only = no motion); current limit too high (overheating) or too low (microsteps lost, weak motor); measuring supply current instead of coil current [1]; VREF set at one VDD then VDD changed; coil pairs mixed; no bulk cap; leaving STEP/DIR floating; stepping faster than the motor can follow without acceleration (stall and buzz); pin-swapping the board in the socket (VMOT into a logic pin destroys it) | |

## 7. Lesson facts

Wiring used in all lessons (Arduino Nano or Uno, 5 V logic): VDD → 5V, GND → GND, STEP → D3, DIR → D4, RST tied to
SLP, VMOT → 12 V (or 3S LiPo 11.1 V nominal, 12.6 V full) with 100 µF across VMOT-GND, motor black/green on 2B/2A,
red/blue on 1A/1B. Optional: EN → D8, MS1/MS2/MS3 → D5/D6/D7.

**Lesson 1, set the current limit, then one revolution in full step.** VREF = 8 × I × 0.068. For a 1.68 A motor
without heat sink, choose I_MAX = 1.0 A → 0.54 V (full-step coil current 0.71 A). MS1-MS3 open = full step = 200
steps/rev, 1.8 deg per step.

```cpp
const int STEP = 3, DIR = 4;

void setup() {
  pinMode(STEP, OUTPUT);
  pinMode(DIR, OUTPUT);
  digitalWrite(DIR, HIGH);
  delay(1);                         // > 1 ms after SLEEP/power-up before the first step
}

void loop() {
  for (int i = 0; i < 200; i++) {   // 200 full steps = 1 rev
    digitalWrite(STEP, HIGH); delayMicroseconds(2500);
    digitalWrite(STEP, LOW);  delayMicroseconds(2500);
  }                                 // 5 ms per step = 200 steps/s = 1 rev/s (60 RPM)
  delay(1000);                      // coils stay energised: holding torque
}
```

**Lesson 2, direction and enable (button).** Button on D2 to GND with `INPUT_PULLUP`; pressing reverses DIR. Change DIR
at least 200 ns before the next STEP rising edge (any digitalWrite gap on an AVR is ~4 µs, so this is automatic).
EN high frees the shaft (no holding torque) while the translator keeps its count.

**Lesson 3, microstepping (correct timing).** MS1 = MS2 = MS3 = HIGH → 1/16 step → 200 × 16 = **3200 pulses/rev**,
1.8/16 = **0.1125 deg** per pulse. Rate = pulses per second; half-period = 1e6 / (2 × rate) µs.

| target speed | microsteps/s at 1/16 | STEP period | half period (HIGH and LOW each) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.25 rev/s (90 deg/s, 15 RPM) | 800 | 1250 µs | **625 µs** |
| 1 rev/s (60 RPM) | 3200 | 312.5 µs | 156 µs |
| the original's 150 µs half period | 3333 | 300 µs | → 1.04 rev/s, not 0.25 rev/s |

```cpp
// MS1, MS2, MS3 tied HIGH = 1/16 step: 3200 pulses per rev
const int STEP = 3, DIR = 4;

void setup() { pinMode(STEP, OUTPUT); pinMode(DIR, OUTPUT); }

void loop() {                        // 800 microsteps/s = 0.25 rev/s = 90 deg/s
  digitalWrite(STEP, HIGH); delayMicroseconds(625);
  digitalWrite(STEP, LOW);  delayMicroseconds(625);
}   // 1.8 / 16 = 0.1125 deg per pulse: smooth and quiet
```

Microstep table [1][6] (MS1 MS2 MS3 → resolution, steps/rev with a 200-step motor, excitation mode):

| MS1 | MS2 | MS3 | resolution | steps/rev | deg/step | Allegro mode |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| L | L | L | full | 200 | 1.8 | 2 phase |
| H | L | L | 1/2 | 400 | 0.9 | 1-2 phase |
| L | H | L | 1/4 | 800 | 0.45 | W1-2 phase |
| H | H | L | 1/8 | 1600 | 0.225 | 2W1-2 phase |
| H | H | H | 1/16 | 3200 | 0.1125 | 4W1-2 phase |

Other MS combinations are not listed in the datasheet table (unverified behaviour; do not teach them). A change of MSx
takes effect at the next STEP rising edge; change modes only at a position common to both modes or position is lost [6].
Microstepping only works when the current limit is low enough that the chopper actually regulates [1].

**Step timing minimums** [6] (Fig. 1, typical): STEP HIGH ≥ **1 µs** (tA), STEP LOW ≥ **1 µs** (tB), setup of
MS1/MS2/MS3/RESET/DIR before the STEP rising edge **200 ns** (tC), hold after it **200 ns** (tD). Wake from sleep:
**1 ms** before the first STEP. The electrical limit (≈500 kHz) is never the real limit: the motor's torque falls with
speed (inductance), so beginners need acceleration.

**Lesson 4 (protocol/library), AccelStepper with acceleration.**

```cpp
#include <AccelStepper.h>
AccelStepper stepper(AccelStepper::DRIVER, 3, 4);   // STEP = D3, DIR = D4

void setup() {
  stepper.setMaxSpeed(800);       // steps/s (full step: 4 rev/s)
  stepper.setAcceleration(1600);  // steps/s^2
  stepper.moveTo(2000);           // 10 rev in full step
}

void loop() {
  if (stepper.distanceToGo() == 0) stepper.moveTo(-stepper.currentPosition());
  stepper.run();                  // call as often as possible
}
```

Wi-Fi variant (ESP32, VDD = 3.3 V): the A4988 accepts 3 V logic; recheck VREF because it scales with VDD [1].

## 8. Internals for the see-through view

- **A4988 QFN**: a silicon die (BiCMOS/DMOS power process) sitting on the exposed copper pad, gold or copper bond wires
  to the 28 perimeter lands, black mould compound, 0.90 mm tall. Most of the die area is the eight large DMOS power FETs
  (two H-bridges); the rest is the translator logic, two current DACs, two comparators, the off-time one-shot, the charge
  pump and the VREG regulator. Pins 3 and 18 (GND) are fused to the paddle for heat [6]. Exact die size and bond-wire
  material: unverified.
- **Sense resistors**: thick-film or metal-element chip resistors (low-ohm, ~1/4-1/2 W class), white (current) or
  green (old) coating; resistive element on an alumina substrate with tinned end caps (element type unverified).
- **Trimpot**: carbon/cermet resistive track on a ceramic or plastic base, metal wiper on the rotor (track type unverified).
- **MLCCs**: stacked ceramic dielectric layers with interleaved nickel electrodes, tinned ends (C1 4.7 µF 1206 is
  X5R/X7R class, voltage rating unverified, must be ≥ 35 V class for VMOT).
- **PCB**: FR-4, 2 layers assumed (unverified), large ground pour under the chip with thermal vias (layout guide [6]).
- **NEMA 17 (hybrid stepper)**: rotor = two toothed soft-iron cups (50 teeth each, offset half a tooth) on either side of an
  axial permanent magnet, on the shaft, two ball bearings in the end caps; stator = laminated steel stack with 8 poles,
  each with small teeth, wound as two phases (coil A on poles 1-3-5-7, coil B on 2-4-6-8). 50 rotor teeth × 4 = 200
  full steps (standard hybrid construction; counts not on the SOYO sheet, unverified for this exact part).

## 9. Assembly and power-up

- Manufacture: all parts are SMD, reflow-soldered on one side [1]. The 1 x 16 header strip ships loose; the user breaks
  it into 2 x 8, pushes it through from the bottom (pins down) and hand-solders on the top [1]. A stick-on heat sink
  (not in #1182's included hardware [1]) goes on the chip **last**, after soldering and cleaning.
- Power-up: no LED lights (the board has none). With VDD up and RST/SLP high, the UVLO releases at VDD ≈ 2.8 V, the
  translator sits at Home, and once VMOT is present the coils are energised at Home (full step: both coils at 70.71 %),
  so the shaft locks and you hear a faint high-pitched chopper hiss/whine (chopper frequency set by tOFF ≈ 12 µs on
  this board; audible components come from the motor). No enumeration; nothing on USB.

## 11. What the original got wrong and what to do instead

- **1/16 microstep sketch timing**: the original used `delayMicroseconds(150)` twice (300 µs per microstep = 3333/s =
  1.04 rev/s) while animating and logging 0.25 rev/s. Correct for 0.25 rev/s: **625 µs** HIGH and 625 µs LOW
  (800 microsteps/s). Sim and code must use the same rate.
- Current-limit text "Vref times a factor printed on the silkscreen": wrong. There is no factor on the silkscreen;
  I_MAX = VREF / (8 × R_CS).
- The original drew the board landscape (20.32 wide, headers H1/H2 along the long edges). That matches reality (two
  1 x 8 headers along the **20.32 mm long edges**, 12.70 mm apart, pins down from the bottom); if drawn landscape, rotate
  this sheet's frame 90 deg and keep the pin order, the 1.27 mm edge offsets and the parts' positions.
- The original's 1 mm corner radius: the Pololu model has square corners.
- The original's "CP" caps block is fine as a group; the board has 7 capacitors (one 1206 + six 0603) and 4 0603
  resistors.
- "Above about 1 A per coil the A4988 overheats": correct per Pololu (~1 A without heat sink or forced air).
- NEMA 17 motor body: 42.3 mm square; length depends on the model (38 mm for #2267, 40 mm for 17HS4401-class, 48 mm for
  #1200). Shaft ⌀5 mm × 24 mm with a D-flat (the original's 12 mm shaft is short). Typical 17HS4401 (StepperOnline):
  1.7 A, 1.5 Ω, 2.8 mH, 40-43 N.cm, ~280 g, 42 x 42 x 40 mm. These come only from third-party listings
  (e.g. https://www.datasheetcafe.com/17hs4401-datasheet-stepper-motor/, https://tinkered.ai/components/stepper-17hs4401s);
  StepperOnline's own page returned 403, and listings disagree on length, resistance and mass, so treat them as
  (unverified). Prefer the SOYO SY42STH38-1684A numbers in 2.5 for the figure, or label the motor generically.
