**What it is.** A small blue PCB that turns a higher DC voltage (for a robot: a 3S LiPo, 9.9–12.6 V) into a lower, adjustable one (5 V for an Arduino). The LM2596 switches its internal transistor on and off 150 000 times a second; a 33 µH inductor and an output capacitor smooth the chopped voltage, and a Schottky diode carries the inductor current while the switch is off. The output voltage is set with a blue 25-turn 3296 trimmer that forms half of the feedback divider. A separate little microcontroller circuit drives a red 3-digit 7-segment display that shows either the input or the output voltage; one push-button toggles between them and two tiny LEDs labelled `IN` and `OUT` show which. It has **no reverse-polarity protection, no input fuse, no low-voltage cut-off and no constant-current mode** [2].

**Corrections to the original Robotics Bench Fig. 12** (details in the sections cited):

1. Board: 66 x **34** mm with **4** corner holes at ≈(2.8, 3.4), (2.8, 31.6), (63.1, 3.4), (63.1, 31.6), not 35 mm with 2 holes (§2.1).
2. Layout: J1 (IN) and J2 (OUT) sit mid-height on the left and right edges, the display along the bottom edge, U1/L1/RV1 along the top (§2.2); the original put J2 at the bottom right and the display at the bottom left.
3. Silkscreen: `2596-SDC`, `IN+`, `IN-`, `OUT+`, `OUT-`, `IN`, `OUT`; there is no "LM2596 DC-DC"/"ADJ" legend on this layout (§2.3).
4. No separate power LED on the voltmeter board; the IN/OUT LEDs and the display show power (§2.2).
5. Caps on this layout are 220 µF **50 V** (input and output); 35 V output caps belong to other variants (§3.3).
6. Current limit: 4.5 A typ / 3.6 A min peak switch current, not 3.0 A (§3.1).
7. Stall: a 2 Ω stalled motor draws 2.5 A at 5 V, below the limit, so the rail does not sag; pick one R_m (§6.1).
8. Nano BOD is 2.7 V (fuse 0xFD); "reset below 4.3 V" is wrong for a stock Nano; ≈3.8 V is the 16 MHz speed-grade edge (§6.1).
9. "Set 12 V" from 11.1 V gives ≈10.2 V at 0.8 A (Fig 7-6), not 10.5 V (§7.1).
10. η = 0.90 is wrong at 5 V out; the datasheet gives ≈80 % (§7.1). Quiz: linear ≈12 W vs buck ≈2.5 W, not ≈1 W.
11. Heat: one Vin for both the stat and the math panel (§7.1).
12. Trimmer position for 5 V is ≈2.5 turns (910°) from the low end, not 800°; 3.3 V is ≈500° (§3.1).

Envistia's spec table says "approx. 53 x 26 x 14 mm" but its own dimensioned photo shows 66 x 34 mm; **trust the photo** (the 53 x 26 figure probably belongs to a different, smaller module; unverified).

## 2. Geometry (board frame)

**Frame.** Component side up, silkscreen `2596-SDC` readable. **The bottom edge is the display/button edge**; the **input terminal block J1 is on the LEFT edge** (`IN+` above, `IN-` below), the **output terminal block J2 on the RIGHT edge** (`OUT+` above, `OUT-` below); the LM2596 tab and the trimmer point to the TOP edge. Origin = bottom-left corner, +x right, +y up, mm.

**How the numbers were obtained.** No design files exist. All positions below were **measured from the straight top-down product photo [2] `-359303.jpg`**: the blue board was segmented by colour (board bbox x 103–671 px, y 257–549 px in the 800 px image → 8.6 px/mm, which reproduces the 66 x 34 mm drawing within 1 %), then a 2 mm grid was overlaid and every part read off it. Expect **±0.5 mm** on centres and ±0.5 mm on sizes; heights are from the part standards or estimated from the angled photos. Every row is therefore "(estimated from photo [2])" unless a datasheet is cited.

**Realistic continuous current.** Worked loss budget at 11.1 V → 5 V (estimates from the datasheet curves; module part values unverified):

| Load | U1 switch loss ≈ VSAT·I·D | D1 loss ≈ VF·I·(1−D) | L1 copper loss (DCR 0.05–0.1 Ω) | U1 junction rise at RθJA 30–50 °C/W |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2 A | 0.97 V x 2 A x 0.52 ≈ **1.0 W** (+ drive/switching ≈0.2–0.4 W) | 0.45 x 2 x 0.48 ≈ 0.43 W | 0.2–0.4 W | **≈35–70 °C** |
| 3 A | 1.16 x 3 x 0.53 ≈ **1.85 W** (+ ≈0.3–0.5 W) | 0.5 x 3 x 0.47 ≈ 0.7 W | 0.45–0.9 W | **≈65–110 °C**, plus the heat D1 and L1 dump into the same small board |

TI's own TO-263 curve (Fig 9-19) shows ≈45 °C junction rise at **2 A**, 12 V → 5 V, on a 9 in² 2-oz board; this module's whole board is 66 x 34 mm ≈ 3.5 in², so it runs hotter. Conclusion, consistent with the vendors [2]: **≈2 A continuous without a heatsink** at room temperature (U1 too hot to touch, ≈70–90 °C case), **3 A only with a stick-on heatsink and airflow**, and the TO-263 has no screw hole, so the "heatsink" is a glued fin on top of the package (RθJC(top) 2 °C/W). Above ≈15 W out a heatsink is mandatory (vendor rule of thumb, unverified source).

**Feedback divider and output voltage** [1] §9.2.2.2.1, with the module values R1 = 330 Ω (FB to GND) and R2 = 10 kΩ trimmer (Vout to FB) [4]:

```
Vout = 1.23 V x (1 + R2 / R1)
R2   = R1 x (Vout / 1.23 - 1)
```

| Target Vout | R2 needed | 3296 turns from the low end (25 turns = 10 kΩ) |
|---|---|---|
| 1.23 V | 0 Ω | 0 |
| 3.3 V | 555 Ω | 1.39 turns (500°) |
| 5.0 V | 1011 Ω | 2.53 turns (910°) |
| 9.0 V | 2085 Ω | 5.2 turns |
| 12.0 V | 2890 Ω | 7.2 turns |
| 38.5 V (pot at max) | 10 kΩ | 25 turns |

So **1.49 V per turn**: going from 3.3 V to 5 V is only 1.1 turns, and 0 → 5 V is 2.5 of the 25 turns, which is why a beginner overshoots. The theoretical top (38.5 V) is above the 37 V advertised; in practice Vout ≤ Vin − dropout. TI wants R1 between 240 Ω and 1.5 kΩ; 330 Ω is inside. **Clockwise raises Vout** (vendor annotation on the photo [2]). If the 3296 is at the high end when delivered, the output simply follows the input ("always equals the input") until you turn it counter-clockwise 10+ turns [3].

Tolerance: ±4 % on VFB alone over temperature, plus the trimmer, so **always set it with a meter**.

### 3.2 Power tree

```
IN+ (J1) ──┬── C1 220 µF 50 V ── GND          (no diode, no fuse, no TVS)
           ├── U1 pin 1 VIN ── [switch] ── pin 2 ──┬── L1 33 µH ──┬── C2 220 µF ── OUT+ (J2)
           │                                        └── D1 SS34 (K) │   R2 10k trimmer ─ FB (pin 4) ─ R1 330 Ω ─ GND
           │                                            (A) GND     │
           └── U2 SOT-89 regulator → meter MCU, DS1, IN/OUT LEDs   └── meter divider (OUT sense)
IN− = GND = OUT− (common, not isolated)
```

- **No reverse-polarity protection** (Envistia: "there is no reverse-polarity protection on this module" [2]).
- **No input fuse, no UVLO, no LiPo low-voltage cut-off.** It will keep draining a battery down to ≈4 V.
- The voltmeter needs **≥4 V on the input** to work [3] (search summary of vendor notes).
- Input and output grounds are the same copper; this is a non-isolated converter.

## 5. Programming and adjustment

No firmware is user-programmable. "Programming" the module means **setting the output voltage**:

1. Connect the source to IN (check polarity). Leave OUT open.
2. Press SW1 until **OUT** is lit.
3. Turn RV1: **clockwise raises Vout**, ≈1.5 V per turn. If nothing happens, keep turning: up to 25 turns end to end; a new pot may be parked at maximum (output = input) [3].
4. Check with a multimeter (the display is ±0.1 V).
5. Power off, connect the load, power on, re-check under load.

The meter MCU's firmware is factory-programmed and closed; no core, no toolchain. Boot behaviour: the display lights as soon as Vin ≥ ≈4 V and shows the input voltage (default, unverified on variant A) with the IN LED on.

## 6. Real failure modes

| Fault | What physically happens | What the user sees |
|---|---|---|
| **Reverse polarity on IN** | Nothing blocks it. C1 is reverse-biased (a reversed electrolytic conducts, heats and eventually vents; how fast at 11 V is unverified); U1's substrate/ESD diode from GND to VIN and the meter regulator conduct directly across the battery, limited only by the source. With a LiPo (tens of amps) U1 usually fails short, and traces or the SS34 may burn. **(Mechanism is the standard IC behaviour; not tested on this module.)** | Display dark, no output, fast heat at U1/C1, hiss or pop from C1, burnt-electronics smell, possibly a bulged can top |
| **Battery on OUT, nothing on IN** (back-feed) | The output cap charges; current back-feeds into the switch node and the meter's OUT divider. Not characterised by TI (output pin abs. min −1 V) **(unverified)** | Usually nothing obvious; do not rely on it |
| **Over-voltage on IN** | >40 V: SS34 (40 V) sees the full input as reverse voltage every switch cycle → leakage, heating, avalanche; >45 V: U1 abs. max, breakdown; >50 V: C1 vents. A 10S LiPo (42 V full) is already too much. | Hot diode, then a dead short or open; may take the load with it if U1 fails short (output = input!) |
| **Over-voltage on the load** (setpoint too high, or setpoint above Vin) | The buck goes to 100 % duty and Vout = Vin − ≈1 V. At 11.1 V in and 0.8 A: ≈**10.2 V** out (Fig 7-6). On a Nano's 5V pin (ATmega328P abs. max 6.0 V): the MCU and USB chip die. | Smoke/heat at the Nano, power LED goes out, sometimes a cracked package |
| **Input brown-out** | No UVLO. As Vin falls below Vout + dropout, Vout follows Vin − ≈1 V; the LM2596 keeps switching down to ≈3.6 V (Fig 7-9), the meter goes dark below ≈4 V. A LiPo is discharged below its safe 3.0 V/cell (9.0 V for 3S) without warning. | Output sags with the battery, display blanks |
| **Over-current / motor stall** | Switch current hits the cycle-by-cycle limit (peak **4.5 A typ, 3.6 A min**); average output current at the limit ≈ ICL − ΔI/2 ≈ 3.3–4.2 A. Vout then falls to I x R_load. Severe overload engages the second stage (frequency fold-back). Sustained >2 A without a heatsink: U1 + D1 + L1 heat the small board until thermal shutdown cycles the output. | Vout sags, display (OUT) drops, U1/L1 too hot to touch, audible whine possible (unverified), output cycles on/off when in thermal shutdown |
| **Output short (5 V to GND)** | Current limit + fold-back holds the switch at ≈4.5 A peaks; the catch diode carries nearly the whole short-circuit current. TI requires the diode to be rated for it; a 3 A (or fake 1 A) SS34 overheats and may fail short or open. U1 eventually thermal-cycles. | OUT reads ≈0.0 V; the meter keeps working because it runs from the input (so the IN reading stays normal); D1 and U1 heat, then possibly a dead module |
| **Cold solder joint** | On a terminal leg: contact resistance → local heating at high current, intermittent output, arcing. **On the trimmer (R2 side / wiper open): FB sees only R1 to ground → 0 V → the regulator goes to 100 % duty → Vout ≈ Vin.** On U1's tab (GND/heatsink): poor heat-sinking → early thermal shutdown. | Flicker/reset of the load; scorched terminal; or the dangerous case: OUT suddenly reads the battery voltage |
| **5 V into a 3.3 V part** | Not a module pin issue, but the classic misuse: the module left at 5 V feeding an ESP32's 3V3 pin (abs. max 3.6 V) or a 3.3 V sensor destroys it. | Hot chip, dead board |
| **3.3 V into a 5 V Nano** | A 16 MHz ATmega328P is outside its safe operating area below ≈3.8 V (linear speed-grade line between 10 MHz @ 2.7 V and 20 MHz @ 4.5 V [6]), but the BOD (2.7 V) does **not** reset it: it may run, glitch or crash. | Random behaviour, not a clean reset |

### 6.1 The stall and the Nano reset (corrected model)

ATmega328P BOD levels [6]: BODLEVEL 110 = **1.8 V**, 101 = **2.7 V (2.5–2.9 V)**, 100 = **4.3 V (4.1–4.5 V)**. The Arduino Nano's default extended fuse is **0xFD = 2.7 V** [7]. So a stock Nano is held in reset only **below ≈2.7 V**; between 2.7 V and ≈3.8 V it is merely out of spec at 16 MHz and can crash or execute garbage (the watchdog is off by default, so a crash does not always reset).

What really happens on a shared 5 V rail when the wheel jams:

1. The motor's back-EMF drops to zero; it becomes a resistor R_m (winding + brushes).
2. If V_set / R_m is **below** the LM2596's limit, the rail **does not collapse**: the regulator just supplies more current. The Nano resets only from the transient (ΔI x ESR of C2 plus loop response) and wiring drop, or later when the module overheats and thermally cycles.
3. If V_set / R_m is **above** the limit, the converter current-limits and the rail falls to V = I_avg,limit x R_m.

Worked numbers (Vin 11.1 V, L 33 µH, 150 kHz, ICL 3.6 A min / 4.5 A typ):

| Stalled motor R_m | Current at 5 V | Rail during stall | Nano (BOD 2.7 V) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2.0 Ω (the original's figure) | 2.5 A < 3.6 A | **stays at 5.0 V** | runs; module heats (2.5 A, no heatsink → thermal shutdown after some time, unverified time) |
| 1.0 Ω | would need 5 A | **3.4–4.2 V** (I ≈ 3.4–4.2 A) | out of spec at 16 MHz, may crash; no BOD reset |
| 0.5 Ω (big motor, or two in parallel) | would need 10 A | **1.7–2.2 V** (I ≈ 3.5–4.3 A) | **below 2.7 V → brown-out reset, again and again** |

The original's `V_out = I_limit x 1.0` with a "2 Ω" motor and a 3.0 A limit is self-contradictory (2 Ω at 3 A is 6 V, more than the 5 V setpoint, so there is no sag at all). **Use one R_m.** For the "Motor stall" mode to produce the brown-out loop it teaches, model **R_m = 0.5 Ω, I_limit = 4.5 A typ (average ≈4.3 A), rail ≈2.2 V**, or keep R_m = 1 Ω and describe it as "out of spec / crash" rather than a BOD reset. (A small yellow TT gearmotor stalls at roughly 1–1.5 A at 5 V, unverified, so a single TT motor will not current-limit this module: say so if the figure draws one.)

Fixes, with numbers:
- **Separate supply for the logic**: feed the Nano's VIN (7–12 V) straight from the 3S pack; the Nano's own LDO keeps 5 V while the buck sags.
- **Hold-up cap behind a diode**: Schottky from the rail to the Nano's 5V pin plus 1000 µF: t = C·ΔV/I = 1000 µF x (4.7 − 3.8 V) / 30 mA ≈ **30 ms** of ride-through (≈67 ms to the 2.7 V BOD).
- Higher BOD (4.3 V fuse) gives a clean reset instead of a crash.

### 6.2 Beginner traps

- Connecting the load before setting the voltage (the pot may be at max: output ≈ input).
- Thinking the pot is broken because the first 10 turns change nothing.
- Reading the display while it shows **IN**, not OUT.
- Asking a buck for more than its input: it only steps **down**; it saturates at Vin − ≈1 V.
- Trusting the ±0.1 V display for a 3.3 V part.
- Running 3 A continuous without a heatsink (rated 2 A continuous [2]).
- No LiPo cut-off: an unattended module flattens a 3S pack.
- `330` on the inductor means 33 µH, not 330 µH.
- Motor and microcontroller on the same output.

## 7. Lesson facts

The module is not programmable, so its lessons run on the **Arduino Nano** (see `docs/facts/nano.md`) powered from it.

| # | Lesson | Facts to use |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Set the rail (Blink on a buck) | Set 5.00 V with no load, then power the Nano from J2 into its **5V pin** (not VIN: VIN wants ≥7 V for the LDO). Blink `LED_BUILTIN` (D13). |
| 2 | Button + sensor: read the rail | The Nano's ADC is 10-bit with AREF = AVcc by default, and AVcc *is* the rail, so a divider from the same rail reads the same count whatever the rail does. Use `analogReference(INTERNAL)` (1.1 V bandgap) with a **47 kΩ / 10 kΩ** divider (5.0 V → 0.877 V → ≈816 counts; full scale 6.27 V), V = counts x 1.1 / 1024 x 5.7 (the bandgap is 1.1 V typical, 1.0–1.2 V part to part [6], so calibrate once against a meter, exactly like the module's own display). Button on D2 with `INPUT_PULLUP` toggles IN/OUT printing, like SW1. |
| 3 | PWM / analog: duty cycle | Ideal buck D = Vout/Vin (45 % at 11.1→5 V); real D = (Vout + Vf)/(Vin − VSAT + Vf) ≈ **52 %** (Vf 0.5 V, VSAT 0.97 V at 2 A). Mimic with `analogWrite(9, 128)` on D9 (Timer1, ≈490 Hz) into an RC filter and compare. The LM2596 runs at 150 kHz; Arduino PWM is 490 Hz (D5/D6: 980 Hz). |
| 4 | Brown-out logger | `Serial.begin(115200); Serial.println(millis());` each 100 ms; the robust sign of a reset is `millis()` restarting from 0 and the banner from `setup()` reappearing. The reset cause lives in `MCUSR`: `PORF` bit 0 = power-on, `EXTRF` bit 1 = reset pin, `BORF` bit 2 = brown-out, `WDRF` bit 3 = watchdog. |

Code for lesson 4 (Arduino AVR core):

```cpp
uint8_t cause;
void setup() {
  cause = MCUSR;          // read before anything clears it
  MCUSR = 0;
  Serial.begin(115200);
  if (cause & _BV(BORF))  Serial.println(F("reset: brown-out"));
  if (cause & _BV(PORF))  Serial.println(F("reset: power-on"));
  if (cause & _BV(EXTRF)) Serial.println(F("reset: reset pin"));
}
void loop() {
  Serial.println(millis());
  delay(100);
}
```

Caveat: on a Nano the bootloader runs first. Optiboot reads MCUSR, clears it and jumps to the sketch, so the sketch often sees **0**; newer Optiboot builds hand the original value over in register r2, which can be captured with a `.init0` hook (`__asm__ ("sts %0, r2" : "=m"(flags))` into a `.noinit` variable). Which bootloader version on a given Nano does which is **unverified**, so the lesson must not depend on BORF; use the `millis()` restart as the observable and treat BORF as a bonus.

### 7.1 Buck vs linear heat, 3S LiPo, 5 V at 2 A (P_out = 10 W)

3S LiPo: 4.2 / 3.7 / ≈3.3 V per cell → **12.6 V full, 11.1 V nominal, ≈9.9 V "empty" under load** (3.0 V/cell = 9.0 V is the hard floor).

Linear loss = (Vin − Vout) x I (input current = output current). Buck loss = P_out/η − P_out; η = **0.80** from the datasheet's 5 V curve (Fig 7-3, ≈80–82 %) and the 70–80 % measured on cheap modules [4]. The original's η = 0.90 is only true at 12–20 V output.

| Vin | Linear (7805-type) heat | Linear input current | Buck heat (η 0.80) | Buck input current | Buck heat if η were 0.90 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 12.6 V | **15.2 W** | 2.0 A | **2.5 W** | 0.99 A | 1.1 W |
| 11.1 V | **12.2 W** | 2.0 A | **2.5 W** | 1.13 A | 1.1 W |
| 9.9 V | **9.8 W** | 2.0 A | **2.5 W** | 1.26 A | 1.1 W |

(A 7805 cannot actually deliver 2 A; L7805 is a 1.5 A part and would thermally shut down. The comparison is for heat only.)

**The original's two inconsistent heat numbers, fixed.** The original showed "Linear would burn 2.7 W" (stat, computed from a sagged 11.06 V) and "2.75 W" (math panel, 11.1 V) for the same 5 V/0.45 A case, and 0.4 W vs 0.48 W in the 12 V case. Use **one Vin variable** for both (11.1 V nominal; the 0.08 Ω battery sag at 0.45 A is only 36 mV). Golden values with one source of truth:

| Mode | Vout | Iout | P_out | Linear heat (11.1 V) | Buck heat | Duty (ideal / real) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Set 5.0 V | 5.00 V | 0.45 A | 2.25 W | **2.75 W** | ≈0.56 W (η 0.80; light-load η unverified) | 45 % / ≈50 % |
| Set 3.3 V | 3.30 V | 0.30 A | 0.99 W | **2.34 W** | ≈0.37 W (η 0.73) | 30 % / ≈36 % |
| "Set 12 V" | **≈10.2 V** (100 % duty, Fig 7-6 at 0.8 A ≈0.9 V drop) | 0.80 A | 8.2 W | 0.72 W | **≈0.72 W** (the saturated switch *is* a linear drop) | 100 % |

And the quiz answer becomes: 11.1 V in, 5 V out at 2 A → **linear ≈12 W, buck ≈2.5 W** (not "≈1 W").

## 8. Internals for the see-through view

- **LM2596 (TO-263-5)**: one bipolar die (≈2–3 mm square, unverified size) soldered to the copper tab/heat spreader, which is also pin 3 GND; bond wires to the five leads (material and count unverified; power pins usually get several parallel wires). On the die (block diagram [1] §8.2): 1.235 V bandgap reference, 2.5 V internal regulator, start-up, transconductance error amplifier with "active capacitor" compensation, PWM comparator, 150 kHz oscillator with frequency-shift input, latch, driver, two current-limit comparators (220 mV and 200 mV sense thresholds), thermal limit, and the large **NPN Darlington "3A SWITCH"** between VIN and OUTPUT. In the ADJ version the internal divider is bypassed (R2 = 0, R1 open), so FB goes straight to the amplifier.
- **L1**: a ferrite drum (bobbin) core wound with ≈20–30 turns of enamelled copper wire (unverified), inside a ferrite sleeve shield; terminals are the wire ends crimped/soldered to tinned clips on the base.
- **D1 SS34**: a silicon Schottky die (metal–silicon barrier) on a lead frame, moulded in black epoxy; a light grey/white band marks the cathode (band colour on this lot unverified).
- **C1/C2**: wound aluminium foil + paper spacer soaked in electrolyte in an aluminium can, rubber bung at the bottom, scored vent cross on top (this is what opens when it is reversed).
- **RV1 3296W**: cermet resistive track on a ceramic substrate, a wiper carried by a nut on a 25-turn lead screw; a slip clutch at each end.
- **DS1**: LED chips (red, GaAsP/AlGaInP class, unverified) in reflector cavities under a diffused epoxy face, one per segment, wired as a multiplexed common-anode or common-cathode matrix (unverified which).
- **Meter MCU**: small 8-bit microcontroller with internal ADC and RC oscillator (type unverified; usually an unmarked/remarked SOP-8 to SOP-16 part).
- No crystal, no RF can, no USB bridge, no flash chip on this module.

## 9. Assembly and power-up

- **Manufacture**: SMD parts (U1, L1, D1, LEDs, SW1, U2, chip R/C) are pasted and reflowed on the top side; then the through-hole parts (J1, J2, C1, C2, RV1, DS1) are inserted and wave- or hand-soldered. The tall caps and screw terminals go on last. The pot is usually left at a random position (often near maximum).
- **Power-up**: no inrush limiting (C1 charges from the battery through the plug: a spark at the XT60/JST is normal); U1 starts switching immediately (the LM2596 has no soft-start pin); output reaches the setpoint in a few ms (unverified exact rise time); the meter boots, DS1 lights with the input voltage and the **IN** LED (default per vendor text, unverified on variant A).

## 10. Uncertainties

- **No design files**: every coordinate is estimated from one top-down product photo (±0.5 mm). Heights are estimated.
- Exact board size: 66 x 34 mm (dimensioned photo) vs 66 x 35.05 ("cutting size" in a listing) vs 66 x 36 mm (case designer) vs Envistia's text 53 x 26 mm. Trust 66 x 34 for variant A.
- The original Robotics Bench used 2 holes at (3, 3) and (63, 31); variant A has **4** holes at ≈(2.8, 3.4)/(63.1, 31.6) corners.
- Inductor part number, saturation current and DCR; diode manufacturer (and whether it is a genuine 3 A SS34); cap ESR.
- Meter MCU type, its regulator part number, its current draw, the display's common-anode/cathode wiring, and whether variant A supports calibration.
- Power-on default display (IN) for variant A.
- Feedback R1 = 330 Ω comes from the HW-411 teardown [4]; it fits the advertised 1.25–37 V range (38.5 V theoretical) but was not read off variant A.
- Thermal shutdown temperature is not in SNVS124G.
- TT motor stall current (≈1–1.5 A at 5 V) is from general knowledge, not a datasheet.
- Reverse-polarity behaviour is inferred from standard IC structure, not tested.
- Light-load efficiency (0.3–0.45 A) is not in the datasheet curves (they are for 3 A).
