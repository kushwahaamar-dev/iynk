# L298N dual H-bridge module (red 43 x 43 mm): fact sheet

## 0. Header

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Product | Generic "L298N Dual H-Bridge Motor Driver" module, the red 43 x 43 mm board sold by almost every Arduino shop (Handson Technology SKU MDU-1049 is one documented reseller; many clones exist, some silk-marked "HW-095" (unverified)) [2] |
| Manufacturer | No single manufacturer: Chinese generic design. No official design files exist. Revision: unnamed. The board modelled here is the one in the Handson user guide photo and schematic [2] |
| Main IC | STMicroelectronics **L298N** (L298 in the **Multiwatt15 vertical** package, order code "L298N") [1]. Many modules carry clone chips with the same marking (unverified) |
| Regulator | **78M05** 5 V / 500 mA linear regulator in DPAK (TO-252), ST logo on the photographed board [2][4] |
| Diodes | 8 x SMA (DO-214AC) rectifiers marked **"M7"** (1N4007-class, 1 A / 1000 V) [2] (marking read from photo; ratings [5] unverified) |

**What it is.** A carrier board for the 1990s ST L298 dual full-bridge driver. The L298N chip stands upright at one edge, bolted to a black
folded heatsink; its two bipolar H-bridges switch the motor supply (marked "+12V", really 5-35 V on this board) onto two 2-screw motor
terminals. Each bridge has two direction inputs (IN1/IN2, IN3/IN4) and one enable (ENA, ENB); on delivery the enables are tied to +5 V
by jumper caps, so the motors run at full speed until you pull a cap and drive the enable with PWM. Eight M7 diodes clamp the motor's
inductive kick to the supply and to ground. A 78M05 linear regulator, enabled by the "5V-EN" jumper, makes the chip's +5 V logic supply
from the motor supply and also brings it out on the third screw of the power terminal. There is no reverse-polarity protection, no fuse,
no current sensing (both sense pins are tied straight to ground), no pull resistors on the inputs and no MCU: it is purely an analog
power stage. Its key weakness, and the point of iynk's "why not to use it" lesson: the bipolar output transistors drop 1.8-3.2 V at 1 A
(4.9 V max at 2 A) [1], so the motor sees much less than the battery and the rest becomes heat.

## 2. Geometry (board frame)

### 2.0 Frame and method

- **Reading orientation:** the only silkscreen text on the top side, **"ENA IN1 IN2 IN3 IN4 ENB"**, reads left to right. In this
  orientation the **heatsink is along the top edge**, the **motor A terminal (OUT1/OUT2) is on the left edge**, the **motor B terminal
  (OUT3/OUT4) on the right edge**, and the **power terminal (bottom-left) and the control header (bottom-right) are on the bottom edge**.
  This matches the original Fig. 06 layout [7].
- Origin = bottom-left corner of the outline, +x right, +y up, mm, component side up.
- **Method:** no official design files exist for this generic board. All coordinates were measured by me from the Handson p. 3 top-view
  photo [2]: board edges found by colour thresholding (red mask spans 493 x 496 px for the labelled 43 x 43 mm, so 11.5 px/mm, about
  ±0.3 mm per measurement), then features picked by eye on a 2x crop and converted with board x = photo-down distance, board y =
  photo-right distance (the photo has the heatsink at the right; the transform is a 90° rotation, not a mirror: the "220 35V VS" print on
  the caps and the ST logo read correctly). The photo is a near-top-down product shot, so parts taller than ~10 mm (heatsink, U1)
  carry about ±1 mm parallax. **Every coordinate in this section is "(estimated from photo [2])"** unless a datasheet is cited.

## 3. Electrical

### 3.1 Schematic (Handson [2], transcribed)

| Net / part | Connection |
|---|---|
| VCC (motor supply, "+12V") | J6-1 → C1 220 µF/35 V to GND → U1 pin 4 **VS** → cathodes of D1-D4 → J8 pin (5V-EN jumper) |
| 78M05 | J8 jumper → U2 Vin (pin 1); U2 Vout (pin 3) = **+5V**; U2 GND (pin 2) = GND |

### 3.3 Saturation voltage and the real drop [1]

| | IL = 1 A | IL = 2 A |
|---|---|---|
| VCEsat(H), source (upper) | 0.95 min / **1.35 typ** / 1.7 max V | **2.0 typ** / 2.7 max V |
| VCEsat(L), sink (lower) | 0.85 / **1.2** / 1.6 V | **1.7** / 2.3 V |
| **Total drop** (upper + lower) | **1.80 min … 3.2 max V** (typ sum 2.55 V) | **4.9 V max** (typ sum 3.7 V) |

Fig. 1 of [1] (VS 42 V, VSS 5 V) starts at 0.8 A: H ≈ 1.25 V, L ≈ 1.1 V (sum ≈ 2.35 V); at 2.4 A H ≈ 2.4 V, L ≈ 2.0 V. Below 0.8 A
there is no data; a bipolar stage does not fall to zero, so use ≈ 2.0-2.2 V at 0.5-0.7 A (extrapolated, unverified).

**Worked numbers for lessons** (typical values; motor voltage = supply − total drop; flyback diodes not involved while driving):

| Supply | Motor current | Drop (typ, range) | Motor sees | Bridge heat (one channel) | Efficiency of bridge |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 7.4 V (2S LiPo) | 0.6 A | ≈ 2.2 V (extrap.) | ≈ 5.2 V | ≈ 1.3 W | ≈ 70 % |
| 7.4 V | 1 A | 2.55 V (1.8-3.2) | **≈ 4.85 V (4.2-5.6)** | 2.55 W | 66 % |
| 12 V | 1 A | 2.55 V | 9.45 V | 2.55 W | 79 % |
| 12 V | 2 A | 3.7 V (≤ 4.9) | 8.3 V (≥ 7.1) | 7.4 W (≤ 9.8 W) | 69 % |

Add the chip's own quiescent loss: IS 13-50 mA from VS plus ISS 7-24 mA from +5 V (e.g. ≈ 0.6 W at 12 V with inputs high).
Two motors at 1 A from 12 V ≈ 5.1 W of bridge heat plus ≈ 0.6 W quiescent. With no heatsink (35 °C/W) that would be > 190 °C rise,
so the heatsink is mandatory; its own thermal resistance is not published (unverified; a guess of 10-15 °C/W would give 55-85 °C rise).

**TB6612FNG comparison** [3]: Vsat(U+L) at 1 A = **0.5 V typ (0.7 max)**, at 0.3 A 0.15 V (0.21); ON resistance ≈ 0.5 Ω upper + lower.
At 7.4 V and 1 A it loses 0.5 W (93 % efficient, motor sees 6.9 V) versus 2.55 W (66 %, 4.85 V) for the L298. But the TB6612 is limited
to VM 13.5 V operating (15 V abs), 1.0 A continuous recommended (1.2 A average, 3.2 A peak), so the L298 still "wins" only on voltage
(46 V) and current (2 A per channel). SMD SSOP-24, no heatsink needed at 1 A.

The original's model (2.0 V drop, 7.4 V → 5.4 V) is the **optimistic edge** (datasheet min at 1 A is 1.8 V). Recommend iynk use
`drop(I) = 2.55 V at 1 A typ`, scaling with Fig. 1, and state "about 2-3 V" in prose; the quiz answer "5.4 V" is acceptable only as
"roughly" next to the distractors 7.4 V and 3.3 V. The audit note O-18 (4.2-5.6 V at 1 A) matches the datasheet.

### 3.4 Power tree and protection

| Input | Range | Protection |
|---|---|---|
| J6 "+12V" (VCC) | Practical **≈ 7-35 V** with 5V-EN on; **5-35 V** with 5V-EN off and +5 V fed externally. Ceiling 35 V from **C1 35 V** and the 78M05's 35 V max [2][4]; the L298 itself allows 46 V [1]. VS must also exceed VIH + 2.5 V ≈ 4.8 V [1] | **None**: no series diode, no fuse, no TVS. C1 is the only bulk cap |
| 78M05 (5V-EN on) | Vin ≤ 35 V; output 500 mA max; dropout ≈ 1.8-2 V typ, so **VCC below ≈ 7 V gives a sagging +5 V rail**; internal current limit and thermal shutdown [4] | Its dissipation is (VCC − 5) x I5V: at 12 V with ≈ 40 mA (L298 logic + LED) 0.3 W; adding an Uno (≈ 50 mA) ≈ 0.6 W; at 24 V with 100 mA, 1.9 W: too hot for a DPAK on small copper. **Hence the seller rule: pull the 5V-EN jumper above 12 V** [6] and feed +5 V into J6-3 from the controller |
| J6 "+5V" | output (5V-EN on) or **input** 4.5-7 V (5V-EN off): powers U1 VSS, the LED and the ENA/ENB jumper pins | none |

- With 5V-EN **off** and nothing on J6 "+5V", the L298 logic is unpowered and **nothing works** (LED off).
- With 5V-EN **on**, do not also push 5 V into J6-3 from another regulator (two regulators fighting; unverified consequence, usually benign
  but avoid).
- **LED D9** (red, unverified colour) on +5 V through R1 1 kΩ: lit = +5 V rail present, ≈ 3 mA. It does not show motor activity.
- **Buttons / switches:** none. Only the three jumpers (5V-EN, ENA, ENB).
- **Sensors:** none. Current sensing is impossible as built (sense pins grounded).

### 3.5 Truth table (one bridge; same for IN3/IN4/ENB) [1] Fig. 6

| EN | IN1 | IN2 | Motor | What the switches do |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| H | H | L | **forward** | OUT1 upper on, OUT2 lower on |
| H | L | H | **reverse** | OUT2 upper on, OUT1 lower on |
| H | H | H | **fast stop (brake)** | both uppers on: motor shorted through the VS side |
| H | L | L | **fast stop (brake)** | both lowers on: motor shorted to GND |
| L | X | X | **free-running stop (coast)** | all four off; current decays through the flyback diodes |

- PWM on **EN** (IN fixed) alternates drive and coast (fast decay through D1-D8). PWM on **IN** with EN high alternates drive and brake.
- Brake current must stay under the 2 A absolute maximum [1].
- [1] also says: drive the enables LOW before switching VS on or off.
- Paralleling for 3 A DC / 3.5 A peak: OUT1 with OUT4, OUT2 with OUT3 (and IN1 with IN4, IN2 with IN3) [1] Fig. 7.

### 3.6 Logic level

5 V logic chip, but thresholds are TTL: VIH 2.3 V, so **3.3 V MCUs (ESP32, Pico, Pi) drive IN/EN directly**. Inputs tolerate up to
VSS / 7 V abs. The module's +5 V output is **not** safe for a 3.3 V GPIO.

## 5. Programming

The module has no firmware. It is driven from any MCU:
- **Arduino UNO / Nano (AVR core arduino:avr 1.8.8 [8])**: `pinMode`, `digitalWrite`, `analogWrite(pin, 0-255)`. PWM pins 3, 5, 6, 9, 10, 11;
  pins 5 and 6 run at ≈ 980 Hz, the others ≈ 490 Hz [8]. All well under the L298's 25-40 kHz commutation limit [1]. Audible whine at
  490 Hz is normal.
- **ESP32 (arduino-esp32 3.x)**: `ledcAttach(pin, freq, bits)` and `ledcWrite(pin, duty)` (3.x API; the 2.x `ledcSetup/ledcAttachPin`
  calls were removed). Version current in October 2026: unverified.
- **MicroPython**: `machine.PWM(Pin(n), freq=1000, duty_u16=...)`.
- **Bipolar stepper**: Arduino built-in **Stepper** library (`Stepper(steps, IN1, IN2, IN3, IN4)`, `setSpeed`, `step`), with both ENA/ENB
  jumpers on [2]. Library version: unverified.
- No upload path, console messages, auto-reset or factory program: none exists on the module.

## 6. Real failure modes

| Fault | What physically happens | What the user sees |
|---|---|---|
| **Reverse polarity on +12V / GND** | No protection. The flyback diodes become a short: GND → D5-D8 (forward) → outputs → D1-D4 (forward) → VCC terminal, two diode drops (≈ 2 V) across the battery (derived from the schematic [2]). C1 is reverse-biased; the 78M05 and L298 substrate diodes also conduct | Battery dumps amps into 1 A diodes: diodes and C1 get hot within seconds, C1 may bulge or vent (pop, fishy electrolyte smell), M7 diodes crack or go short; LiPo wires get hot. LED stays off. A blown diode later shows as a dead short across the supply |
| **Reverse polarity on the +5V screw** (5V-EN off) | Reverse 5 V on VSS and C2 (no protection) | C2 heats; L298 logic likely damaged (unverified) |
| **Over-voltage** | > 35 V: 78M05 input (if 5V-EN on) and C1 (35 V) over-rated. > 46/50 V: L298 | 78M05 fails, often short, sending VCC onto +5 V and killing the Arduino on that rail; C1 bulges/vents. 12-35 V with 5V-EN on: the 78M05 runs hot (finger-burning), then thermal-shuts-down, so the +5 V rail and the LED flicker off and on |
| **Brown-out** | No UVLO in the L298 [1]. VSS below 4.5 V is out of spec; 78M05 needs ≈ 7 V in (dropout ≈ 2 V [4]) | With a 2S pack sagging under a motor start, the +5 V rail dips; an Arduino powered from J6-3 resets (UNO brown-out detector 2.7 V, efuse 0xFD in the AVR core [8]) and the motors twitch on every reboot. A 6 V (4 x AA) supply gives only ≈ 4 V logic with 5V-EN on |
| **Over-current / stall** | No current limit (sense pins grounded). > 2 A DC exceeds abs max [1]; only the overtemperature protection acts | A stalled motor makes the heatsink too hot to touch within a minute; the chip hits thermal shutdown and the motor stutters (cuts out, restarts as it cools). Repeated, or a dead short on an output, can fuse the bond wires/output transistors: one direction or one channel dies (unverified detail) |
| **Output shorted (OUT1 to OUT2 or to GND)** | No short-circuit protection documented [1] | Large current, possible instant failure before thermal shutdown (unverified) |
| **Cold solder joint** | U1's 15 leads and the screw-terminal pins carry amps; a cracked joint arcs or opens with vibration | Motor cuts out on bumps, one direction missing, a terminal pin that gets hot and browns the PCB |
| **5 V into a 3.3 V pin** | Taking +5V from J6-3 to an ESP32/Pico 3V3 pin or GPIO | Destroys the 3.3 V MCU (the module's inputs are fine at 3.3 V, its 5 V output is not) |
| **Shorting +5V to GND** | 5V-EN on: the 78M05 current-limits and thermal-shuts-down [4]; 5V-EN off: the source (Arduino 5V, USB) supplies the short (UNO's 500 mA PTC trips) | LED goes out, regulator hot, L298 logic off so motors stop; recovers after removing the short |
| **Beginner traps** | (1) **No common ground** between Arduino and module: inputs float relative to the chip, motors ignore commands or twitch. (2) **ENA/ENB jumpers pulled but EN left unconnected**: enable floats (no pull resistor, undefined per [1]); motors do not run (or run erratically: unverified). (3) **PWM into ENA with the jumper still on**: the jumper ties ENA to +5V, so the MCU pin is shorted to 5 V when it drives LOW (pin over-current) and speed never changes. (4) **6 V motor on a 6 V supply**: motor sees ≈ 3.5-4 V. (5) **5V-EN on above 12 V**: hot 78M05. (6) **Expecting current sensing**: sense pins are grounded. (7) **Powering motors from the Arduino 5 V pin**: Handson's example does it [2] but USB/regulator cannot supply stall currents; brown-outs. (8) **Slow M7 diodes**: [1] asks for fast diodes (trr ≤ 200 ns); M7 are standard-recovery (unverified spec) and can run warm at high PWM rates |

## 7. Lesson facts

Wiring used below (common and in [6]): **ENA → D9, IN1 → D8, IN2 → D7, ENB → D3, IN3 → D5, IN4 → D4**, module GND → Arduino GND,
battery + → "+12V", 5V-EN on (battery ≤ 12 V). D9 and D3 are 490 Hz PWM pins on the UNO/Nano [8].

**Lesson 1: Direction (digital I/O).** Leave both EN jumpers on (full speed). `digitalWrite(IN1, HIGH); digitalWrite(IN2, LOW);` forward,
swap for reverse, both LOW (or both HIGH) brakes. Point: two pins = four switches. Swapping the motor wires is the same as swapping IN1/IN2.

**Lesson 2: Button + sensor.** Button on D2 to GND with `pinMode(2, INPUT_PULLUP)` (reads LOW when pressed) toggles direction; a
potentiometer on A0 (`analogRead`, 0-1023 on the UNO's 10-bit ADC) picked up in lesson 3 for speed. Note the module has no sensor of its own.

**Lesson 3: PWM speed, brake vs coast.** Pull the ENA cap, wire ENA to D9:

```cpp
const int ENA = 9, IN1 = 8, IN2 = 7;

void setup() {
  pinMode(ENA, OUTPUT);
  pinMode(IN1, OUTPUT);
  pinMode(IN2, OUTPUT);
}

void loop() {
  digitalWrite(IN1, HIGH);
  digitalWrite(IN2, LOW);
  int speed = map(analogRead(A0), 0, 1023, 0, 255);
  analogWrite(ENA, speed);          // PWM on EN: drive / coast
}
```

Brake: `digitalWrite(IN1, HIGH); digitalWrite(IN2, HIGH);` with ENA high. Coast: `analogWrite(ENA, 0)` (or `digitalWrite(ENA, LOW)`).
Show that the motor does not start until a fairly high duty (the 2-3 V bridge drop plus motor stiction; the exact threshold depends on
the motor: unverified).

**Lesson 4: Protocol / Wi-Fi.** Either serial commands on the UNO (`Serial.begin(115200)`, parse "F 200" / "R 120" / "S"), or an ESP32
driving the module directly at 3.3 V logic (VIH 2.3 V [1]), with `ledcAttach(ENA_PIN, 20000, 8)` for a quiet 20 kHz PWM (below the
L298's 25 kHz typ / 40 kHz max fc [1]) and a `WebServer` page. Keep 5V-EN on and do **not** connect the module's +5V to the ESP32 3V3 pin;
feed the ESP32 by USB or via its 5V/VIN pin.

**"Why not to use it" lesson numbers:** drop 1.8-3.2 V at 1 A, ≤ 4.9 V at 2 A [1]; TB6612 0.5 V at 1 A [3]; 7.4 V pack → ≈ 4.85 V at the
motor at 1 A (≈ 5.2 V at 0.6 A); heat 2.55 W per motor at 1 A vs 0.5 W; L298 is 46 V / 2 A capable, TB6612 13.5 V / 1 A. Modern
alternatives: TB6612FNG, DRV8833 (DRV8833 specs not checked this run).

## 8. Internals for the see-through view

- **L298N die**: a single bipolar/power die on the Multiwatt copper tab (the tab is GND, pin 8) [1]; each bridge = 4 NPN-type power
  switches (upper "source" and lower "sink") with logic gates (AND of IN and EN) per the block diagram [1]. The lower emitters of each
  bridge join at the Sense pin. Bond wires from die pads to the 15 leads (die size and wire count unverified). No flyback diodes inside:
  that is why the module has D1-D8 [1].
- **78M05**: monolithic linear regulator die in DPAK: NPN pass transistor (Darlington), bandgap reference, error amplifier, current limit,
  thermal shutdown [4]; tab = GND.
- **M7 diodes**: a single silicon PN rectifier die (glass-passivated, typical) between two clips in an SMA epoxy body (unverified).
- **Electrolytics**: rolled aluminium foil + paper spacer soaked in electrolyte, rubber bung at the base, aluminium can with vent scoring
  on the top (standard construction).
- **Heatsink**: plain folded aluminium (or steel, unverified) sheet, black finish, screwed to the tab; no thermal pad visible.
- No crystal, no RF, no MCU, no flash on this board.

## 9. Assembly and power-up

- **Manufacture** (inferred from the parts): SMD first by reflow (78M05 DPAK, R1, D9, 8 x SMA diodes, the two SMD electrolytics on their
  plastic bases), then through-hole by hand or wave: pin headers, the three blue screw terminals, and the L298N already screwed to its
  heatsink (its 15 leads are pre-formed into two rows). Jumper caps go on last.
- **Power-up** (battery on "+12V", 5V-EN on): C1 charges, the 78M05 brings up +5 V, **the power LED lights**. With ENA/ENB caps on and all
  IN pins floating or LOW, the motors do nothing (LOW/LOW = brake). With IN floating the behaviour is not specified by [1]. Nothing
  enumerates, no program runs.
