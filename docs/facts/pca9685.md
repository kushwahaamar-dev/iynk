**What it is.** A 62.2 x 25.4 mm blue breakout that carries one PCA9685: a chip with its own ~25 MHz RC oscillator, sixteen
12-bit PWM generators and an I2C slave interface. The host writes an ON count and an OFF count (0-4095) for a channel once
over I2C and the chip then keeps producing that waveform on its own, so 16 servos or LEDs run from two wires (SDA/SCL)
with no CPU time. Logic power (VCC, 2.3-5.5 V, from the microcontroller) is separate from servo power (V+, from a blue
screw terminal through a reverse-polarity-protection P-MOSFET, "6V Max" per the bottom silkscreen). Every output has a
220 ohm series resistor and goes to a 3-pin servo header column (PWM / V+ / GND). Six solder jumpers set the I2C address
0x40-0x7F and identical 6-pin headers on both short edges let boards be chained (up to 62 boards, 992 outputs) [1][2][3].

## 3. Electrical

### 3.1 Controller (PCA9685PW) [3]

| item | value |
|---|---|
| Function | 16 LED/PWM outputs, each a 12-bit (4096-step) ON/OFF counter compare; I2C Fm+ slave up to 1 MHz |
| Clock | **internal 25 MHz oscillator, "typical"**. The datasheet gives **no min/max** for it. Adafruit: "In theory the internal oscillator (clock) is 25MHz but it really isn't that precise ... a range between about 23-27MHz" and their servo example calibrates with `setOscillatorFrequency(27000000)` [4]. Optional EXTCLK pin (DC-50 MHz) — on this board **EXTCLK (pin 25) is tied to GND**, so the internal oscillator is always used [2] |
| Supply VDD | 2.3-5.5 V; abs max 6.0 V; IDD 6 mA typ / 10 mA max (no load, 1 MHz SCL); standby 2.2 µA typ; power-on reset 1.70 V typ |
| I/O voltage | all inputs/outputs **5.5 V tolerant** (abs max VI/O = 5.5 V) even at VDD = 3.3 V |
| Output drive | per pin abs max 25 mA; sink 25 mA typ at VOL 0.5 V; source 10 mA (VOH ≥ 4.0 V at VDD 4.5 V); totem-pole by default (MODE2 OUTDRV = 1), open-drain selectable |
| Package total | ISS **400 mA** max, Ptot 400 mW |
| PWM frequency | 24 Hz (PRE_SCALE 0xFF) to 1526 Hz (PRE_SCALE 0x03, the hardware minimum); power-up default PRE_SCALE 0x1E = 200 Hz |
| Power-up state | MODE1 = 0x11 (SLEEP = 1, oscillator off; ALLCALL = 1). All LEDn_OFF_H have the full-OFF bit set, so **every output is LOW** until the host programs it (register defaults: MODE1 SLEEP = 1*, LEDn_OFF_H[4] full OFF = 1*, MODE2 OUTDRV = 1* totem pole, OUTNE = 00* [3]) |
| Prescale equation | `prescale = round( osc_clock / (4096 × update_rate) ) − 1` (Eq. 1 [3]); PRE_SCALE can only be written while MODE1 SLEEP = 1 |

### 3.2 The 50 Hz servo numbers

| quantity | 25 MHz (nominal) | 27 MHz (Adafruit example calibration) |
|---|---|---|
| prescale for 50 Hz, datasheet Eq. 1 | round(122.07) − 1 = **121** | round(131.84) − 1 = 131 |
| prescale computed by library 3.0.3 `setPWMFreq(50)`: `(uint8_t)((osc/(freq*4096)) + 0.5 − 1)` [4] | 121 | 131 |
| real frame | 4096 × 122 / 25 MHz = **19.988 ms = 50.03 Hz** | 4096 × 132 / 27 MHz = 20.025 ms = 49.94 Hz |
| one tick | 122 / 25 MHz = **4.880 µs** | 132 / 27 MHz = 4.889 µs |

Ticks = pulse / tick (setPWM(ch, 0, ticks)). At 25 MHz / prescale 121: 500 µs → 102.5, 544 → 111.5, 600 → 123.0,
**1000 → 204.9 (205)**, **1500 → 307.4 (307)**, **2000 → 409.8 (410)**, 2400 → 491.8. `writeMicroseconds()` truncates
(it passes a double into a uint16_t), so `writeMicroseconds(1000)` writes 204, not 205 [4].

**What an uncalibrated chip does.** If the library assumes 25 MHz but the chip really runs at 27 MHz, prescale stays
121, so the real tick is 122/27 MHz = 4.519 µs: the frame becomes 18.5 ms (54.0 Hz) and `setPWM(ch, 0, 307)` produces
1.387 ms instead of 1.500 ms (−7.5 %). On an SG90 at ≈10 µs/° that is about 11° off centre. This is why
`setOscillatorFrequency()` exists: measure the 50 Hz frame on a scope, adjust until it reads 50 Hz [4].

Fig. 08 check: the original's `ticks = round(pulse × 4096 / 20000)` and `map(deg, 0, 180, 205, 410)` are correct for an
ideal 25 MHz oscillator (205/256/307/358/410) [11]. It never mentions the oscillator error or calibration.

### 3.3 Power tree [2]

```
J1 pin1 (PWRIN) ──► Q1 AOD417 drain(tab) ─ source ──► "5.0V" net = servo V+ rail
J1 pin2 ─────────────────────────── GND          │       ├─ V+ row of all 16 servo columns
Q1 gate ── GND                                     │       ├─ C2 + pad (footprint only)
                                                   │       └─ pin 1 "V+" of JP3 and JP4 (side headers)
JP3/JP4 pin2 "VCC" ──► U1 VDD (pin 28), C1 10 µF, LED1 anode, R2 SDA/SCL pull-ups (10 kΩ), SJ_Ax upper pads
```

- **V+ (servo power).** Enters through J1 only if you want protection. Q1 is a P-MOSFET with drain on the input, source
  on the rail and gate grounded: with correct polarity the body diode conducts first, VGS ≈ −V+ turns the channel on
  (RDS(on) < 55 mΩ at VGS = −4.5 V [7]), so a 5 V supply loses only ~0.05 V per amp. VDS max −30 V, VGS max ±20 V,
  ID −25 A [7]. Silkscreen limit **"V+: 6V Max"** (that limit protects the servos; nothing on the rail but servos and
  C2). Adafruit power options: 5 V 2 A or 10 A switching supplies, 4 x AA (6 V alkaline, 4.8 V NiMH) [5].
- **The side header V+ pin (JP3/JP4 pin 1) is on the protected side**, after Q1, and bypasses the protection: "Side
  breakout pins are not!" [2]. Chained boards share V+ through these pins.
- **VCC (logic).** 3-5 V (silk "VCC: 3-5V"), from the microcontroller's 5 V or 3.3 V pin. Powers only U1, the LED and
  the pull-ups. "+5v -> VCC (this is power for the BREAKOUT only, NOT the servo power!)" [5]. **V+ and VCC are not
  connected on the board.**
- **There is no regulator, no polyfuse and no diode on VCC.**
- Logic level: the PCA9685 accepts 3.3 V or 5 V logic and is 5.5 V tolerant [3]. **SDA/SCL are pulled up to VCC
  (10 kΩ, R2)**, so the bus idles at whatever VCC is. With a 3.3 V MCU (ESP32, Pi, Pico) power VCC from 3.3 V.
- Output HIGH level = VCC (3.3 V or 5 V) through 220 Ω. SG90s accept a 3.3 V signal (unverified for every unit).

### 3.4 Current limits

| path | limit |
|---|---|
| Any PWM output | 25 mA abs max per pin, 10 mA rated source current [3]. The on-board 220 Ω caps a HIGH output shorted to GND at ≤ 5 V / 220 Ω = 22.7 mA (less in practice, the driver's own resistance adds), so a short on the header does not exceed the 25 mA rating |
| All outputs together | 400 mA package [3] |
| Servo V+ rail | set by the supply, the J1 terminal and the copper; Q1 is rated −25 A continuous at VGS = −10 V [7] (less at the −5 V it gets from a 5 V rail). Terminal and trace ratings are not published (unverified) |
| VCC draw | ≈ 6-10 mA (U1) + ≈ 6 mA LED at 5 V (estimate: (5 − ~2.1 V) / 470 Ω) + pull-up current |

| Buttons / switches | none |
| Sensors | none |
| OE | active-LOW output enable (U1 pin 23), pulled **low by R7 10 kΩ** so outputs are enabled by default; drive OE HIGH to switch all 16 outputs off at once (MODE2 OUTNE default: outputs LOW when OE = 1 [3]) |

### 3.6 I2C address

7-bit address = `1 A5 A4 A3 A2 A1 A0` = **0x40 + (A5·32 + A4·16 + A3·8 + A2·4 + A1·2 + A0)**. Each Ax pin is pulled to
GND by a 10 kΩ array resistor (R1/R2) and the solder jumper connects it to VCC, so **open = 0, bridged = 1**
("(Open=0/Closed=1)") [2]. All open = **0x40** (library default `PCA9685_I2C_ADDRESS 0x40` [4]); A0 bridged = 0x41; A1 =
0x42; A0+A1 = 0x43; A2 = 0x44 [5]; all six = 0x7F.

Reserved: **0x70 = LED All Call** (on at power-up, ALLCALLADR default 0xE0 = 0x70 7-bit) — every PCA9685 on the bus
answers it, so never jumper a board to 0x70 (A5 + A4 bridged). The Software Reset general call (0x00 then data 0x06) is
the other address the datasheet excludes. "64 possible ... leaving a maximum of 62 addresses" [3] → 62 boards x 16 = 992
outputs [1].

Register map used by the library [3][4]: MODE1 0x00, MODE2 0x01, LED0_ON_L 0x06 (channel n at 0x06 + 4n: ON_L, ON_H,
OFF_L, OFF_H), ALL_LED_ON_L 0xFA … ALL_LED_OFF_H 0xFD, PRE_SCALE 0xFE.

## 5. Programming

- Arduino IDE 2.x; library **"Adafruit PWM Servo Driver Library" 3.0.3** (2026-03-03) [4], which pulls in **Adafruit
  BusIO 1.17.4** [9]. `#include <Wire.h>` and `#include <Adafruit_PWMServoDriver.h>`. The board has no firmware: only
  the host is programmed (avrdude to the Nano, see `nano.md`).
- Python: CircuitPython / Blinka `adafruit_pca9685` and `adafruit_motor.servo` (versions unverified).

API (library 3.0.3 header [4]):

| call | what it does |
|---|---|
| `Adafruit_PWMServoDriver pwm;` / `Adafruit_PWMServoDriver(0x41)` / `Adafruit_PWMServoDriver(0x40, Wire)` | construct (default 0x40, default `Wire`) |
| `bool begin(uint8_t prescale = 0)` | starts I2C, `reset()` (writes MODE1 RESTART), sets the oscillator to 25 MHz, then **`setPWMFreq(1000)`**; returns false if nothing ACKs |
| `void setOscillatorFrequency(uint32_t freq)` / `getOscillatorFrequency()` | tells the library the real clock (call **after `begin()`**, because begin resets it to 25 MHz) |
| `void setPWMFreq(float freq)` | clamps 1-3500, computes prescale, sleeps, writes PRE_SCALE, wakes, sets auto-increment |
| `uint8_t setPWM(uint8_t num, uint16_t on, uint16_t off)` | writes the 4 registers of channel num (0-15); returns 0 on success |
| `void setPin(uint8_t num, uint16_t val, bool invert = false)` | duty 0-4095 with proper full-on/full-off handling (for LEDs) |
| `void writeMicroseconds(uint8_t num, uint16_t us)` | reads PRE_SCALE and converts µs to ticks using the stored oscillator value |
| `uint8_t readPrescale()`, `uint16_t getPWM(num, off)`, `sleep()`, `wakeup()`, `setOutputMode(bool totempole)`, `setExtClk(prescale)`, `reset()` | utilities |

The Adafruit servo example's constants: `SERVOMIN 150`, `SERVOMAX 600` (ticks), `USMIN 600`, `USMAX 2400`,
`SERVO_FREQ 50`, `pwm.setOscillatorFrequency(27000000)` [4]. (At 50 Hz / 25 MHz, 150 ticks is 732 µs, not 600 µs; the
"600" comment is left over from an older 60 Hz example. Unverified history, but the arithmetic is exact.)

Serial behaviour: none from the board. `begin()` returning false (or an I2C scanner finding nothing at 0x40) is the only
error signal. An I2C scanner on a correctly wired single board reports **0x40 and 0x70** (All Call also ACKs)
(unverified for every scanner sketch; follows from [3]).

## 6. Real failure modes

| fault | what physically happens | what the user sees |
|---|---|---|
| **V+ reversed at J1** | Q1's body diode is reverse-biased and VGS is positive, so the FET stays off; no current flows to the servo rail | nothing moves, nothing heats; green LED still on (it is on VCC). Fix by swapping the wires |
| **V+ reversed on the side-header V+ pin** | bypasses Q1; −V across every servo and C2 (if fitted). Servo electronics can be destroyed; a fitted electrolytic C2 reverse-biased will heat and may vent | possible servo death, hot/bulging C2, electrolyte smell, a pop |
| **C2 fitted backwards** (user adds the bulk cap) | reverse-biased electrolytic on V+: leakage, heating, venting | bulge, hiss, pop, fishy smell. The original's "C1 stripe back" fault [11] maps to **C2** here — real but only if the user fits a cap |
| **VCC reversed** | no protection; current through U1's ESD/substrate diodes and the LED is reverse-biased | U1 can be destroyed; LED dark; supply may current-limit (unverified magnitude) |
| **Over-voltage on V+** (> 6 V, e.g. 7.4 V 2S LiPo or 12 V) | Q1 (30 V) and the board are fine; the SG90 (rated 4.8 V nominal, commonly 4.8-6 V) is overstressed | servo buzzes hot, may burn out; smell of hot motor |
| **Over-voltage on VCC** (> 5.5 V; abs max 6.0 V) | U1 out of spec, then destroyed | board stops ACKing |
| **Brown-out when servos share the Arduino 5 V** (V+ wired to the Nano 5V pin) | SG90 inrush/stall (≈ 0.36-0.55 A each per [10]; several hundred mA when moving [5]) pulls through the Nano's USB path (500 mA PTC + Schottky, see `nano.md`); the 5 V rail sags; the ATmega328P BOD trips at 2.7 V typ (2.5-2.9 V) and resets the Nano; the PCA9685 keeps running unless VCC falls below its 1.70 V POR | servos twitch, the Nano reboots in a loop (bootloader blink on L, sketch restarts, serial port drops/garbage), USB may disconnect; PWR LED flickers |
| **Servo stall** (horn blocked, arm overloaded) | SG90 draws its stall current (≈ 0.36-0.55 A, third-party [10]; no manufacturer figure) continuously; motor and drive transistors heat; plastic gears can strip | buzzing, warm case, jitter; on a weak supply the rail sags and other servos jitter |
| **Over-current on a PWM pin** | 220 Ω limits it; pin is 25 mA abs max [3] | usually nothing breaks; an LED lights directly from a channel without an extra resistor |
| **Servo plug reversed** (brown on PWM) | signal pin sees GND, servo GND sees the PWM output; 220 Ω limits the output current | servo does nothing; no damage (this is why the 220 Ω resistors exist [1]) |
| **5 V into a 3.3 V MCU** | with VCC = 5 V the 10 kΩ pull-ups hold SDA/SCL at 5 V; an ESP32/Pi/Pico pin (not 5 V tolerant) sees 5 V through 10 kΩ (≈ 0.17 mA into its clamp diode) | may work for a while, stresses the MCU; fix: power VCC from 3.3 V. The PCA9685 side is always fine (5.5 V tolerant) |
| **5 V to GND short** | on VCC: the Nano's USB PTC (500 mA hold / 1 A trip) heats and trips, or the host port shuts down; on V+: the supply's limit decides (a battery can deliver many amps: hot wires, melted header plastic) | Nano dies/ resets, PTC warm; with batteries, smoke possible |
| **Cold solder joint** | on a servo header pin: intermittent signal or power; on JP3 SDA/SCL: intermittent I2C | servo jitters or drops out when touched; `begin()` fails randomly; I2C lock-up |
| **SDA/SCL swapped** | no START condition recognised | scanner finds nothing; `begin()` returns false |
| **Two boards at the same address** | both ACK and both drive the same data; reads collide | both boards do the same thing; fix: bridge A0 on one (0x41) [5] |
| **Board jumpered to 0x70** | collides with All Call of every PCA9685 | all boards respond to writes for that board |
| **No common ground** (separate servo supply, GND not shared) | it **is** shared through the board: J1 GND, header GND and servo GND are the same net [2] | — (a beginner trap only if V+ and its GND are wired straight to the servos, bypassing the board) |
| **Forgetting `setPWMFreq(50)`** | `begin()` leaves the chip at ~1000 Hz [4]; a 1 ms frame cannot carry a 1-2 ms servo pulse | servos twitch, ignore commands or run hot |
| **Not calibrating the oscillator** | see 3.2: up to ≈ ±8 % pulse error | servos off-centre by several degrees; end-of-travel commands hit the stops and buzz |

## 7. Lesson facts

Use channel **0** and channel **1** (the original's bench servos) on block JP2, servo power from 4 x AA (6 V alkaline)
or a 5 V ≥ 2 A supply into J1, VCC from the Nano 5V, SDA/SCL on A4/A5.

**SG90 pulse-to-angle model.** The datasheet: "Position "0" (1.5 ms pulse) is middle, "90" (~2 ms pulse) is all the way
to the right, "-90" (~1 ms pulse) is all the way to the left" [6]. In practice hobby-servo software uses wider limits for
0-180°: the Arduino Servo library maps 0-180° onto **544-2400 µs** [8] and Adafruit's servo example uses **600-2400 µs**
[4]. **Trust the libraries for full travel** (they are what real SG90s are driven with) and treat the datasheet's
1-2 ms as the conservative, guaranteed band. iynk model: centre 1500 µs, ≈ 10 µs per degree (600 µs = 0°, 2400 µs =
180°), dead band 10 µs [6]; units vary and the end stops differ by a few degrees (unverified, per-unit). With the
original's 1.0-2.0 ms mapping a real SG90 swings only about 100°, not 180°.

| angle | µs (10 µs/° model) | ticks @ 25 MHz, prescale 121 |
|---|---|---|
| 0° | 600 | 123 |
| 45° | 1050 | 215 |
| 90° | 1500 | 307 |
| 135° | 1950 | 400 |
| 180° | 2400 | 492 |

SG90 other facts [6]: 9 g; 22.2 x 11.8 x 31 mm approx. (31 mm includes the output shaft; body with mounting tabs is
≈ 32 mm long, unverified); stall torque 1.8 kgf·cm at 4.8 V; 0.1 s/60°; 0-55 °C; translucent blue case (TowerPro
genuine; colour from common product photos, unverified); leads **brown (GND), red (+), orange (signal)**, female 3-pin
0.1" plug (the original's "black / red / orange" ribbon [11] is the Futaba-style colouring; TowerPro uses brown).
Current: ≈ 10 mA idle, 100-250 mA moving, ≈ 360-550 mA stalled at 5 V [10] (third-party measurements; no manufacturer
figure). **The original's "a stalling servo pulls about 1 A" is right for standard servos such as the MG996R, too high
for an SG90**; two SG90s stalling at once (~0.7-1.1 A) are still enough to brown out a USB-powered Nano.

**Lesson 1 — I2C bring-up and centring (Blink/I-O equivalent).**

```cpp
#include <Wire.h>
#include <Adafruit_PWMServoDriver.h>

Adafruit_PWMServoDriver pwm(0x40);   // A0-A5 open

void setup() {
  Serial.begin(115200);
  if (!pwm.begin()) {
    Serial.println("PCA9685 not found at 0x40");
    while (true) {}
  }
  pwm.setOscillatorFrequency(27000000);  // calibrate per chip with a scope
  pwm.setPWMFreq(50);
  pwm.writeMicroseconds(0, 1500);        // centre before fitting the horn
  pwm.writeMicroseconds(1, 1500);
}

void loop() {}
```

**Lesson 2 — button + pot (sensor) to servo.** Pot wiper on A0 (Nano/Uno), button on D2 with `INPUT_PULLUP` (active
LOW):

```cpp
int us = map(analogRead(A0), 0, 1023, 600, 2400);
pwm.writeMicroseconds(0, us);
if (digitalRead(2) == LOW) pwm.writeMicroseconds(1, 1500);
```

**Lesson 3 — PWM: sweep and LED dimming.** Sweep with `for (int us = 600; us <= 2400; us += 10) { pwm.writeMicroseconds(0, us); delay(20); }`
and back down. LED on channel 15 (220 Ω already on board): `pwm.setPWMFreq(1000); pwm.setPin(15, 2048);`. Note one chip
has **one frequency for all 16 channels**: servos (50 Hz) and flicker-free LEDs (≥ 200 Hz) on the same board is a
compromise.

**Lesson 4 — protocol: I2C addressing and chaining.** Scan the bus (expect 0x40 and 0x70), bridge A0 on the second board,
chain JP4 → JP3 with a 6-wire cable, then:

```cpp
Adafruit_PWMServoDriver pwm1 = Adafruit_PWMServoDriver(0x40);
Adafruit_PWMServoDriver pwm2 = Adafruit_PWMServoDriver(0x41);
```
[5]. Two-joint arm: shoulder on channel 0, elbow on channel 1, 2-link IK in the host.

## 8. Internals (see-through view)

- **U1 PCA9685PW:** a CMOS die in a TSSOP-28 moulded body on a copper lead frame, gold or copper bond wires to the 28
  gull-wing leads (bond-wire material unverified). On the die: I2C Fm+ interface with input filters, power-on reset,
  the **25 MHz RC oscillator** (no quartz anywhere on this board), an 8-bit prescaler, a 12-bit counter, sixteen 12-bit
  ON/OFF compare registers and output drivers, an OE gate [3].
- **Q1 AOD417:** a P-channel trench MOSFET die soldered to the DPAK tab (the tab is the drain), source and gate bond
  wires to the two leads [7] (internal construction typical, unverified).
- **Resistor arrays:** four thick-film resistors per 0603x4 chip on an alumina substrate, convex terminations.
- **C1:** multilayer ceramic (MLCC) stack.
- **LED1:** an InGaN/AlGaInP green die (chemistry unverified) on a lead frame under clear epoxy.
- **J1:** a moulded plastic body with two brass/steel rising-clamp boxes and captive screws, each soldered by one pin.
- **SG90:** DC motor, nylon/plastic 4-stage gear train, a potentiometer on the output shaft as the position sensor, and a
  small control PCB (pulse-width comparator + H-bridge) (standard micro-servo construction; SG90 internals unverified).

## 9. Assembly and power-up

- Adafruit ships the SMD side **fully assembled and tested** (reflow: U1, Q1, arrays, C1, R7, R10, LED1). Loose in the
  bag: **4 x 3x4 male straight headers, one 2-pin 3.5 mm terminal block (blue or black), one 1x6 0.1" header** [1][2].
  **No capacitor** [5]. The user solders: the 1x6 header into JP3 (left), the four 3x4 blocks, the terminal block last
  (tallest). Optionally a second 1x6 header into JP4 for chaining and a bulk electrolytic into C2 (≈ 100 µF per servo:
  "470uF or more for 5 servos", rated above V+, + pad toward Q1) [5]. The original's 12-part build order [11] is
  plausible except that **C2 is optional and not in the kit**, and **JP4 needs a header you supply**.
- Power-up with VCC: the **green LED lights immediately**; U1 comes out of POR asleep (oscillator off, all outputs LOW),
  so a servo plugged in sees no pulses and is limp (an analog servo with no signal does not hold position).
- `begin()` wakes it at ~1000 Hz; `setPWMFreq(50)` sets 50 Hz; the first `setPWM`/`writeMicroseconds` makes the servo
  drive to position. Servo power must be present on V+ for anything to move; the LED gives no hint either way.
- No enumeration, no default program.

## 10. Uncertainties

- Pinguin-font revision (Dec 2022+): silkscreen font and possibly wording differ; no public design file. Positions assumed
  unchanged.
- PCB thickness (1.6 mm assumed), component heights (standard packages), terminal-block height and wire-entry direction
  (photo-estimated).
- Mask colour hex is an estimate from the photo.
- PCA9685 oscillator tolerance: no datasheet spec; "about 23-27 MHz" is Adafruit's library comment, not NXP.
- SG90 stall current: third-party only (360 mA measured, ~550 mA quoted). Full-travel pulse range: per unit; the 600-2400
  µs model follows the libraries, the datasheet says ~1-2 ms. SG90 case colour and lead colours from common genuine units.
- CircuitPython library versions not checked.
- The I2C scanner showing 0x70 follows from the datasheet (All Call enabled at power-up); not tested on hardware.
- Behaviour of the PCA9685 outputs during an Arduino reset loop (servo twitch from `begin()`'s 1000 Hz) is inferred
  from the library source, not measured.
