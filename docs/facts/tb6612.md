**What it is.** A tiny square board that carries one Toshiba TB6612FNG, which holds two independent H-bridges built from low-resistance LDMOS transistors. Each bridge drives one brushed DC motor forwards, backwards, short-brake or coast. Two logic pins (IN1, IN2) set the mode, and a PWM pin chops the bridge to set the speed. Motor power (VM, 2.5 to 13.5 V) and logic power (VCC, 2.7 to 5.5 V) are separate supplies with a common ground. The whole chip sleeps when STBY is low. The board adds only three capacitors (0.1 µF on VM, 0.1 µF on VCC, 10 µF tantalum on VM). Every chip pin goes to two 1x8 0.1 in headers spaced 0.6 in apart, so the board straddles a breadboard channel like a DIP-16. It has **no LED, no resistor, no regulator, no reverse-polarity protection and no mounting holes** [4][5].

**Corrections to the original Robotics Bench Fig. 05** (robotics-bench-a.md section 7):
- The size is **20.32 x 20.32 mm**, not 20.3 x 25.4 mm. The headers run **down the left and right edges** (vertical columns), not along the top and bottom edges.
- There is **no R1 and no power LED D1**. The board has four parts: U1, C1, C2, C3 [4].
- **C1 (0.1 µF) is on VM and C2 (0.1 µF) is on VCC.** The original has them the other way round. The board nets are authoritative [4].
- C3 is a **10 µF tantalum chip capacitor, EIA 3528 (case B)**. It is not a 5 mm tall radial electrolytic. It is about 2 mm high (see 2.3).
- The silkscreen has no "+" by C3. The polarity mark is a silk bar on the anode (VM) side [4].
- The motor-side header order is VM, VCC, GND, A01, A02, B02, B01, GND, top to bottom. The original's order matches. The control-side order, top to bottom, is **PWMA, AIN2, AIN1, STBY, BIN1, BIN2, PWMB, GND**, which also matches [4].
- The drop is **not a constant**. It is I x about 0.5 Ω (both switches together, typical): **about 0.15 V at 0.3 A, about 0.25 V at 0.5 A, 0.5 V typical (0.7 V max) at 1 A** [1]. See section 3.3 for how the original's 0.2 V and 0.5 V figures fit together.
- The original's "coast" mode sends IN1 = IN2 = LOW with PWM = 0. The datasheet defines Stop (coast, outputs off) only for **IN1 = IN2 = L with PWM = H**. For a defined coast, write PWM HIGH (`analogWrite(PWMA, 255)`) [1]. See 3.4.

Heat check: P = I² x R per channel. At 1 A that is 1 x 0.5 = 0.5 W per channel, so both channels at 1 A give **about 1 W**. That is more than the 0.78 to 0.89 W rating, so the chip heads for thermal shutdown. Pololu warns it "can get hot enough to burn you long before the chip overheats" [7].

### 3.3 On-resistance and voltage drop (the 0.5 V vs 0.2 V question)

Datasheet [1], Ta = 25 °C, VCC = VM = 5 V. **Vsat(U+L) is the total drop across the upper and lower switch together**, which is the whole bridge loss seen by the motor:

| Load current | Vsat(U+L) typ | max |
|---|---|---|
| **0.3 A** | **0.15 V** | 0.21 V |
| **0.5 A** | **≈ 0.25 V** (not tabulated: 0.5 Ω x 0.5 A, interpolated) | ≈ 0.35 V (from 0.7 Ω, interpolated) |
| **1.0 A** | **0.5 V** | 0.7 V |

The feature list gives "Output low ON resistor; 0.5 Ω (upper+lower Typ. @VM ≥ 5 V)". The two table rows agree: 0.15 V / 0.3 A = 0.5 Ω and 0.5 V / 1 A = 0.5 Ω. The worst case is 0.7 Ω.

**Reconciliation.** Both numbers in the original are right at different currents. Fig. 05's "0.5 V" is the **1 A** figure. Fig. 06's "about 0.2 V" matches about **0.3 to 0.4 A**. A TT gear motor running at 6 to 7.4 V typically draws 0.1 to 0.3 A, so its real drop is **about 0.05 to 0.15 V**. Its stall current (about 1 A or more) gives 0.5 V or more **(TT motor current typical, unverified)**.

**What SimLab must use:** `Vdrop = I_motor x 0.5 Ω` (typical). Clamp at the 1 A operating limit, and model thermal shutdown above that. In text, write: "about 0.5 V at 1 A (about 0.15 V at 0.3 A), against about 2 V for the L298N's bipolar switches". Take the L298 figure from the L298N fact sheet. ST's L298 datasheet gives a total drop of roughly 1.8 V typ / 3.2 V max at 1 A **(from memory, unverified here)**.

Note that the brief's checklist line RBA-372 ("must say about 0.5 V") is only right at 1 A. Prefer the current-dependent wording above.

### 3.4 Control logic (datasheet [1], "H-SW Control Function")

| IN1 | IN2 | PWM | STBY | OUT1 | OUT2 | Mode |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| H | H | H/L | H | L | L | **Short brake** |
| L | H | H | H | L | H | **CCW** |
| L | H | L | H | L | L | Short brake |
| H | L | H | H | H | L | **CW** |
| H | L | L | H | L | L | Short brake |
| L | L | H | H | OFF (high-Z) | OFF (high-Z) | **Stop** (coast) |
| H/L | H/L | H/L | **L** | OFF (high-Z) | OFF (high-Z) | **Standby** |

- **PWM low means short brake, not coast.** With PWM, each period alternates drive and brake (both low-side switches on, which is slow decay). Speed is therefore close to linear in duty.
- IN1 = IN2 = L with PWM = L is **not listed** in the datasheet table. For a defined coast, hold PWM HIGH **(behaviour with PWM low unverified)**.
- "CW" and "CCW" are Toshiba's names for OUT1 high or OUT2 high. Which way a motor turns depends on how it is wired. Call them forward and reverse in lessons, with that caveat.
- **Dead time** (shoot-through protection, designed value): **50 ns** H to L and **230 ns** L to H. Output rise time tr is 24 ns typ and fall time tf is 41 ns typ.
- **PWM frequency: 100 kHz max** (fPWM, operating range). There is no minimum. Arduino Uno/Nano `analogWrite` runs at 490 Hz (pins 3, 9, 10, 11) or 977 Hz (pins 5, 6), which is fine but audible. 20 kHz or more is silent.
- **Logic thresholds:** VIH ≥ 0.7 x VCC (max VCC + 0.2 V), VIL ≤ 0.3 x VCC (min -0.2 V). STBY uses the same levels.
- **Every input (AIN1, AIN2, PWMA, BIN1, BIN2, PWMB, STBY) has an internal 200 kΩ pull-down to GND.** Input current is 15 µA typ (5 to 25) at VIN = 3 V. A floating STBY therefore means standby, and floating inputs mean stop or brake.

### 3.5 Logic levels and 5 V tolerance
- The input thresholds track **VCC**. So **power VCC from the controlling MCU's logic rail** (5 V for an Uno/Nano, 3.3 V for an ESP32, Pico or micro:bit).
- **3.3 V MCU with VCC = 5 V: not guaranteed.** VIH min = 3.5 V is above 3.3 V. It often works at room temperature but is out of spec.
- **5 V MCU with VCC = 3.3 V:** within the 6 V absolute maximum for inputs, but above the VCC + 0.2 V operating limit. Out of spec, and current may flow through input protection into VCC **(internal input structure not published; unverified)**.
- Motor outputs swing between GND and VM, and they are not logic signals.

### 3.6 Internal diodes
Each output has regenerative (freewheel) diodes to VM (upper) and to PGND (lower). These are the LDMOS body diodes **(body-diode construction inferred from the LDMOS structure)**. VF(U) and VF(L) are **1.0 V typ, 1.1 V max at IF = 1 A** [1]. No external flyback diodes are needed. During coast and dead time, motor current flows back into VM through these diodes, so VM rises if the supply cannot sink current ("Back-EMF" note, datasheet p. 10).

### 3.7 LEDs, buttons, sensors
**None.** The board has no LED (the original's green power LED and R1 do not exist), no buttons and no sensors [4].

| R | 6 | BIN2 (silk BI2) | 16 | Channel B input 2 | 0 to VCC | 200 kΩ pull-down |
| R | 7 | PWMB | 15 | Channel B PWM | 0 to VCC | 200 kΩ pull-down |
| R | 8 | GND | | Ground | 0 V | |

Paralleling (Pololu) means tying AIN1 to BIN1, AIN2 to BIN2, PWMA to PWMB, AO1 to BO1 and AO2 to BO2, which gives 2 A continuous [8]. SparkFun's layout puts B02 above B01, so paralleling with jumpers needs care.

## 5. Programming

Nothing on the board is programmable. It is driven by any MCU.
- **Arduino:** plain `pinMode` / `digitalWrite` / `analogWrite`. Optional library: **"SparkFun TB6612FNG Motor Driver Library" v1.0.0** (`#include <SparkFun_TB6612.h>`, `architectures=avr`) [10]. API: `Motor(int In1pin, int In2pin, int PWMpin, int offset, int STBYpin)`, `drive(speed)` with speed -255 to 255 (it also sets STBY HIGH), `drive(speed, ms)`, `brake()` (IN1 = IN2 = HIGH, PWM 0), `standby()` (STBY LOW), and the helpers `forward(m1, m2, speed)`, `back(...)`, `left(...)`, `right(...)`, `brake(m1, m2)`. `stop()` and `coast()` are not implemented [9][10].
- **ESP32 (arduino-esp32 core, latest release 3.3.12, 2026-09-18):** `analogWrite()` works through LEDC. For silent motors use the 3.x API `ledcAttach(pin, 20000, 8)` and `ledcWrite(pin, duty)`. The 2.x API `ledcSetup`/`ledcAttachPin` was removed in 3.0.
- **MicroPython (Pico/ESP32):** `from machine import Pin, PWM`; `pwm = PWM(Pin(n)); pwm.freq(20000); pwm.duty_u16(32768)`.
- **gpiozero (Raspberry Pi, gpiozero 2.0.1):** the documented parameters are `Motor(forward, backward, *, enable=None, pwm=True)`. There are two correct options. (a) The explicit way: `DigitalOutputDevice` for AIN1, AIN2 and STBY (STBY `initial_value=True`) plus `PWMOutputDevice(PWMA, frequency=20000)`. This gives drive/short-brake PWM, exactly as on Arduino. (b) `Motor(forward=AIN1, backward=AIN2, enable=STBY)` with PWMA tied HIGH (to 3V3 or a `DigitalOutputDevice` held on). gpiozero then PWMs the IN pin, so the off phase is IN1 = IN2 = L, which is **Stop/coast (fast decay)**, not brake. Speed is less linear, but it is correct and safe.
- There is no auto-reset, boot behaviour, firmware or factory sketch.

## 6. Real failure modes

| Fault | What physically happens | Which part | What the user sees |
|---|---|---|---|
| **VM reversed** (SparkFun) | No protection. Current flows from GND through the lower body diodes to each output, then through the upper body diodes to VM: two diode drops in series, so the battery is nearly shorted through the die. **C3 (tantalum) is reverse-biased**, and tantalums fail short, often violently. | U1 die, C3 | Large current from the battery, U1 and C3 hot within seconds, burnt-epoxy smell, possibly a cracked or blackened C3 or chip. The board is usually dead. |
| VM reversed (Pololu #713) | Reverse-protection MOSFET blocks it [7] | protection FET | Nothing happens. Works when corrected. |
| **VCC reversed** | No protection on either board [7]. Substrate and ESD diodes conduct from GND to VCC. | U1 | The MCU's rail is loaded heavily, U1 warms, and it is likely damaged. |
| **Over-voltage on VM** (> 15 V abs; e.g. a 4S LiPo at 16.8 V, or back-EMF pumping) | Output LDMOS avalanche or breakdown. C3's voltage rating may be exceeded (**rating unknown**). | U1, C3 | Sudden failure: a short from VM to GND, the supply sagging or its fuse tripping, sometimes a popped package. A 3S LiPo (12.6 V full) is inside the 13.5 V limit but leaves little margin for braking spikes. |
| **Over-voltage on VCC** (> 6 V) | Logic damage | U1 | Erratic or dead control |
| **Brown-out of VCC** | Below about 1.9 V UVLO turns the outputs off, and they recover above about 2.2 V [1] | UVLO | Motors stop. Recovery is silent. |
| **VM sagging** (weak battery, or motor start from shared supply) | Below 4.5 V the output current is derated to 0.4 A. The common real failure is the **MCU** browning out when motors share its supply (Nano/Uno BOD about 2.7 V), not the TB6612. | MCU | MCU resets and the motor jerks or restarts in a loop |
| **Over-current / stall** (> 1.2 A avg, > 3.2 A pulse) | No current limit. I²R heating up to TSD at 175 °C, which turns the outputs off. It restarts at about 155 °C and cycles. A hard short or a big stall beyond 3.2 A can destroy outputs faster than TSD reacts [1] ("TSD does not necessarily protect ICs under all circumstances"). | U1 | The chip is too hot to touch, and the motor pulses on and off every second or so. A dead channel after abuse. |
| **Cold solder joint on a header** | Intermittent contact. On STBY, the 200 kΩ pull-down drops the chip into standby whenever the joint opens. On VM or GND, the motor cuts out. On a PWM pin, the channel brakes. | header joint | Motors stutter when the board is touched or vibrates. A dull, grainy, cone-shaped joint. |
| **5 V into a 3.3 V-logic setup** | See 3.5: inputs are 6 V abs max, so they survive, but it is out of spec. If the motor VM is accidentally put on VCC (e.g. 7.4 V on VCC), the logic is destroyed. | U1 | 5 V logic: usually works. VM on VCC: dead chip, possibly hot. |
| **3.3 V MCU with VCC = 5 V** | VIH = 3.5 V is not met | — | Random or no response, worse when warm |
| **Shorting 5 V to GND** | The board has no 5 V rail. If VCC (the MCU's 5 V) is shorted to GND, the MCU's supply goes down. On a Nano or Uno on USB, the 500 mA polyfuse trips. | MCU board fuse | Everything goes dark. The fuse recovers after cooling. |
| **Output shorted to GND/VM or A01 to A02** | High current through one LDMOS, then TSD | U1 | Hot chip, channel cycling |
| **Beginner traps** | (1) STBY left unconnected: nothing moves (pull-down). (2) No common ground between MCU and motor supply. (3) Motors powered from the Arduino 5 V pin: resets. (4) PWM wired to a non-PWM pin: full speed or stop only. (5) Expecting PWM LOW to coast: it brakes. (6) VM below 4.5 V: weak torque, 0.4 A limit. (7) Motor leads on A01 and B01 instead of A01 and A02. (8) Confusing silk "A01" (zero) with datasheet "AO1" (letter O). (9) ROB-14451 bought without headers, with pins pressed in unsoldered. | | |

## 7. Lesson facts

Wiring used throughout (Arduino Nano or Uno, matching the original Fig. 05): **PWMA = D5, AIN1 = D7, AIN2 = D8, STBY = D9, PWMB = D6, BIN1 = D10, BIN2 = D11**. VCC goes to the Nano's 5V, GND is common, and VM goes to the battery (e.g. 2S LiPo 7.4 V or 4 x AA 6 V). D5 and D6 are Timer0 PWM at about 977 Hz. SparkFun's own example uses AIN1 2, AIN2 4, PWMA 5, BIN1 7, BIN2 8, PWMB 6, STBY 9 [9].

**Lesson 1, on/off and direction (digital I/O):**
```cpp
const int PWMA = 5, AIN1 = 7, AIN2 = 8, STBY = 9;
void setup() {
  pinMode(PWMA, OUTPUT); pinMode(AIN1, OUTPUT); pinMode(AIN2, OUTPUT); pinMode(STBY, OUTPUT);
  digitalWrite(STBY, HIGH);              // LOW (or unconnected) = standby
  digitalWrite(PWMA, HIGH);              // full on, no PWM yet
}
void loop() {
  digitalWrite(AIN1, HIGH); digitalWrite(AIN2, LOW);  delay(2000); // CW
  digitalWrite(AIN1, HIGH); digitalWrite(AIN2, HIGH); delay(500);  // short brake
  digitalWrite(AIN1, LOW);  digitalWrite(AIN2, HIGH); delay(2000); // CCW
  digitalWrite(AIN1, LOW);  digitalWrite(AIN2, LOW);  delay(1000); // stop: coast (PWM stays HIGH)
}
```
Always write both IN pins explicitly. Never rely on the 200 kΩ pull-down (audit O-19).

**Lesson 2, button + sensor:** a push button on D2 to GND with `pinMode(2, INPUT_PULLUP)` (pressed = LOW) toggles direction. Debounce with `millis()`. Sensor: a potentiometer wiper on A0 sets speed, or an HC-SR04 / IR sensor stops the motor when something is near.

**Lesson 3, PWM speed (analog in, PWM out):**
```cpp
int speed = map(analogRead(A0), 0, 1023, 0, 255);
digitalWrite(AIN1, HIGH); digitalWrite(AIN2, LOW);
analogWrite(PWMA, speed);    // PWM low phase = short brake, so speed is roughly linear in duty
```
The average motor voltage is about `duty x (VM - I x 0.5 Ω)`. At 7.4 V, 50% duty and 0.2 A, that is about 3.65 V.

**Lesson 4, protocol:** Serial commands (`Serial.begin(115200)`, then parse "F 128", "R 160", "B" for brake, "C" for coast, "S" for standby) or ESP32 Wi-Fi. On an ESP32, VCC = 3V3, and any output-capable GPIO works (not the input-only GPIO34 to 39 on a classic ESP32). Use `WebServer` (ESP32 Arduino core) with `ledcAttach(PWMA, 20000, 8)`. Library alternative: SparkFun TB6612 v1.0.0 (AVR only per `library.properties`) [10].

MicroPython (Pico, VCC = 3V3):
```python
from machine import Pin, PWM
stby = Pin(9, Pin.OUT, value=1); ain1 = Pin(7, Pin.OUT); ain2 = Pin(8, Pin.OUT)
pwma = PWM(Pin(5)); pwma.freq(20000)
ain1.value(1); ain2.value(0); pwma.duty_u16(32768)  # CW at 50%
```

## 8. Internals (for the see-through view)

- **U1 TB6612FNG:** one monolithic silicon die made in Toshiba's Bi-CD (bipolar + CMOS + DMOS) process [1]. It holds:
  - **two H-bridges**, each made of **four LDMOS power transistors** (two upper and two lower, eight in total). Each has an intrinsic body diode, which acts as the freewheel diode.
  - CMOS control logic A and B, with the IN1/IN2/PWM decode and dead-time generator.
  - a STBY block, a UVLO comparator on VCC, and a thermal-shutdown sensor (TSD) [1] (block diagram).
  - **200 kΩ pull-down resistors** on all seven inputs.

  The die sits on the SSOP24's copper lead-frame paddle, with bond wires to the 24 leads. The outputs and power grounds are **doubled** (AO1 x2, AO2 x2, BO1 x2, BO2 x2, PGND1 x2, PGND2 x2) and VM has three pins (VM1, VM2, VM3), which shares the current. The bond-wire material (gold or copper) and die size are **unverified**. Whether the upper switches are N- or P-channel is not published **(unverified)**.
- **C3 tantalum:** a sintered porous tantalum pellet (the anode, +) with a tantalum-pentoxide dielectric grown on it. The cathode is manganese dioxide (or a conductive polymer), with graphite and silver layers, a lead-frame, and a moulded epoxy body. The band marks +. The specific construction of SparkFun's part is **unverified**.
- **C1, C2 0402 MLCCs:** stacked ceramic dielectric layers (X5R/X7R class, **unverified**) with interleaved nickel electrodes and tin-plated end terminations.
- **PCB:** 2-layer FR-4, about 1.57 mm. Top and bottom copper carry the VM, GND and output traces.
- There is **no** crystal, regulator, USB bridge, flash, RF can or LED.

## 9. Assembly and power-up

- **Manufacturing:** single-sided SMD. Solder paste is stencilled on top, then U1, C1, C2 and C3 are placed and reflowed. There are no bottom-side parts. On ROB-14450 the two 1x8 male headers are fitted last (through-hole, hand or selective solder). ROB-14451 ships bare [3][6]. The Pololu #713 ships with a loose 1x16 strip to break into two 1x8 headers [7].
- **Build order for a figure:** reflow U1 → C1/C2 → C3 → headers last.
- **Power-up:** nothing visible happens, because there is no LED. With VCC applied and STBY low (or floating), the chip draws < 1 µA from each rail and the outputs are high-Z. When STBY goes HIGH, ICC rises to about 1.1 to 1.5 mA, and the outputs follow IN1/IN2/PWM within tens of ns (tr 24 ns, tf 41 ns). Above about 2.2 V on VCC, UVLO releases [1]. There is no default program.

## 10. Uncertainties

- The official Toshiba URL (rev. 2014-10-01) was blocked (HTTP "Access Denied"). The figures come from the 2008-05-09 revision mirrored by Pololu. I do not expect the electrical tables to differ, but this is **unverified**.
- **Solder-mask and silk colours** (red/white) come from product photos, not from the files.
- **Heights** of the 0402s, the tantalum C3 and the header plastic are typical package values, not SparkFun BOM values. C3's voltage rating and brand are unknown. The schematic gives only "10uF".
- U1's exact laser-marking layout and the bond-wire material are unverified.
- **Pololu #713 pin order and board geometry** were not extracted. Pololu publishes no design files, and the product page text does not list the order.
- The behaviour with IN1 = IN2 = L and PWM = L is not specified in the datasheet table. The datasheet's "Without PWM Operation" remark for the 0.4 A low-VM row is ambiguous in layout.
- The internal input structure is unpublished, so the effect of 5 V into a 3.3 V-powered chip is inferred.
- The TT-motor running and stall currents (0.1 to 0.3 A, about 1 A) are typical, not measured. The L298 drop (about 1.8 to 3.2 V at 1 A) is from memory; take it from the L298N fact sheet.
- Versions were checked on 2026-10-24: arduino-esp32 3.3.12 (GitHub latest release), gpiozero 2.0.1.post3 (PyPI), and the SparkFun TB6612 library 1.0.0 (`library.properties` on master). The gpiozero `Motor` parameter list was checked against the stable docs. Its exact PWM-on-IN behaviour comes from the docs' description of `pwm=True`.
