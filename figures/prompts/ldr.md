# Light sensor, LDR (Colorfigures prompt)

Make a colourful, hand-inked isometric figure of a **GL5528 cadmium-sulphide photoresistor (LDR)** standing in two
holes (e8, e10) of a white mini breadboard on a pale green cutting mat, with a flat blue cast shadow.

- A 5.1 mm ceramic disc about 2 mm thick in a glossy epoxy coat, on two tinned legs about 3.4 mm apart that splay
  to the holes. The face looks up: two silver comb electrodes with an orange-red CdS track snaking between them.
- Non-polar. Resistance from the maker's table: 10-20 kΩ at 10 lux (a dim room, not daylight), at least 1 MΩ in
  the dark, gamma 0.6, so R = 15 kΩ × (E / 10 lux)^-0.6: 100 lux 3.8 kΩ, 1000 lux 0.95 kΩ, daylight about 240 Ω.
  It reacts in about 20 ms to light and 30 ms to dark.

What it does:

- **Light level ▾** (0, 1, 10, 100, 1000, 10 000, 100 000 lx) and a press on the part, which steps through covered,
  dim room, room, desk lamp and daylight. A warm glow grows on the face as the light rises; covered, the face
  darkens. The label reads "LDR1 · 300 lx · 5.2 kΩ".
- **See-through** clears the epoxy: the ceramic base, the comb electrodes and the track, with blue charge carriers
  drifting along it, more of them the more light there is. Labels: CdS track, Comb electrode, Ceramic base.
- In the usual divider (LDR from 5 V to A0, 10 kΩ to GND) more light gives a higher reading. Wired straight across
  5 V in daylight it takes 21 mA and 105 mW, over its 100 mW rating.

Look: warm paper, heavy ink outline with finer inner lines, flat bands, mustard accent, the five palettes.
