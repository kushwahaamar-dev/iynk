# Force sensor, FSR 402 (Colorfigures prompt)

Make a colourful, hand-inked isometric figure of an **Interlink FSR 402 force-sensing resistor** standing in two
neighbouring holes (e8, e9) of a white mini breadboard on a pale green cutting mat, with a flat blue cast shadow.

- A thin (about 0.45 mm) tan polymer film: a round head 18.3 mm across with a dark 12.7 mm active area printed with
  fine interdigitated silver fingers, on a 7.6 mm tail with two silver traces, about 56 mm long overall. The tail
  ends in two male crimp pins on 2.54 mm pitch, gripped by a small black clincher housing, so it plugs straight
  into the breadboard and stands up.
- Non-polar. Unloaded it is an open circuit (over 10 MΩ). From about 0.2 N it conducts: 0.2 N 30 kΩ, 1 N 6 kΩ,
  10 N 1 kΩ, 100 N 250 Ω (log R against log F between those points). Rated about 0.2-20 N: "how hard", not a
  scale.

What it does:

- **Force (N) ▾** and a press on the head: a fingertip presses the active area (about 10 N) until you let go, and
  the active area darkens as the resistance falls. The label reads "FSR1 · 10 N · 1 kΩ" or "no force · open".
- **See-through** parts the three layers: the top film with its silver fingers, the spacer ring with the air vent
  running down the tail, and the bottom film with the dark force-sensing ink; under force an amber patch shows where
  the films touch, growing with the force. Labels: Silver fingers, Spacer, air vent, Force-sensing ink.
- In the usual divider (FSR from 5 V to A0, 10 kΩ from A0 to GND) 1 N reads about 3.1 V and 10 N about 4.5 V;
  without the pull-down A0 floats while nobody presses.

Look: warm paper, heavy ink outline with finer inner lines, flat bands, mustard accent, the five palettes.
