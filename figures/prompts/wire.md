# Jumper wire (Colorfigures prompt)

Make a colourful, hand-inked isometric figure of **three male-to-male jumper wires** arcing over a white mini
breadboard (17 columns, rows a-j, no power rails) on a pale green cutting mat (`figures/parts/wire.js`, drawn by the
Board Bench engine that every lesson circuit uses): a red one from a2 to j9, a black one from c4 to h14 and a yellow
one from b12 to f16, the first two crossing the centre channel.

- Each wire is a round 22 AWG lead in an arch: it rises 7 mm above its ends plus about a fifth of its length, so
  longer wires arch higher.
- A black 2.5 mm square housing, 4.5 mm tall, at each end, with a short tinned pin into the hole.
- The arch is cut into short pieces so it sorts correctly against the parts round it from every angle.

What it does:

- It has no pickers. In lessons the same wire runs from a board's header pin (it plugs into the female header from
  above) to a breadboard hole, in any of ten colours: red, black, orange, yellow, green, blue, white, purple, brown,
  grey. The breadboard solver treats it as a perfect join between its two ends.
- Each wire carries its name for screen readers and the host bridge ("Wire from a2 to j9").

Look: warm paper, heavy ink outline with finer inner lines, flat light/mid/dark bands, mustard accent, the five
Colorfigures palettes. The mat and breadboard fade out round the wires, which the page frames.
