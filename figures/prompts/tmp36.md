# Temperature sensor, TMP36 (Colorfigures prompt)

Make a colourful, hand-inked isometric figure of an **Analog Devices TMP36** analog temperature sensor in a TO-92
package, standing in three neighbouring holes (e8, e9, e10) of a white mini breadboard on a pale green cutting mat,
with a flat blue cast shadow.

- Black epoxy, the TO-92 "D" shape: a 4.8 mm flat face toward the viewer, straight sides, a half-round back, 3.7 mm
  deep and 4.8 mm tall, marked "TMP" / "36GZ" in pale grey on the flat face.
- Three tinned legs leave the body 1.27 mm apart and are formed out to the 2.54 mm holes. Flat face toward you,
  legs down: +Vs on the left, Vout in the middle, GND on the right.
- Supply 2.7-5.5 V (7 V absolute maximum), under 50 µA. Vout = 0.5 V + 10 mV per °C: 0.5 V at 0 °C, 0.75 V at
  25 °C, 0.1 V at −40 °C, 1.75 V at 125 °C (it works to 150 °C, 2.0 V, less accurately), ±2 °C typical.

What it does:

- **Temperature (°C) ▾** and a press on the part, which pinches it between two fingers (33 °C) until you let go.
  The label reads "U1 · 25 °C · 0.75 V".
- **Put in backwards ▾**: with +Vs and GND swapped it conducts through its substrate. The epoxy flushes red and heat
  shimmer rises off it ("backwards: burning hot"); once it has died it is dark, cracked across the face and
  smoking ("destroyed").
- **See-through** clears the epoxy: the lead frame, the paddle on the middle lead with the silicon die (a bandgap
  temperature core and an output amplifier) coloured from cold blue to hot red by its temperature, and gold bond
  wires to the outer leads. Labels: Silicon die, Gold bond wires, Lead frame.

Look: warm paper, heavy ink outline with finer inner lines, flat bands, mustard accent, the five palettes.
