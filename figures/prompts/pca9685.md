# Fig. 08 — PCA9685 servo arm (Robotics Bench, Colorfigures prompt)

Two figures share this prompt: the module on its own (`figures/modules/pca9685.js`) and the bench that builds a desk
arm around it (`figures/robotics/pca9685.js`). Facts in `docs/facts/pca9685.md`.

Make a colourful, hand-inked isometric figure of the **Adafruit 16-Channel 12-bit PWM/Servo Driver** (product 815, rev
C): a blue 62.23 x 25.40 mm board with 3.175 mm corner radii and four 2.5 mm plated holes, every part at its Eagle
position. Along the bottom edge four black 3 x 4 servo header blocks (channels 0-15, each column PWM / V+ / GND from the
chip side); along the top the blue V+/GND screw terminal, the AOD417 P-MOSFET (D417) that protects it, a 470 µF
electrolytic in the C2 footprint and the six address jumpers A0-A5; the 6-pin I2C header on the left edge (V+, VCC,
SDA, SCL, OE, GND), its unfitted twin on the right. The PCA9685PW in TSSOP-28 in the middle, 220 Ω arrays in front of
it, the 10 kΩ pull-ups, the green power LED. Silkscreen from rev C: "16 x 12-bit PWM", "I2C Address (Open=0/Closed=1)",
the channel numbers.

What it does (module page):

- **Programs** are what a host sketch makes the chip do: "Centre the servos" (1500 µs = 307 ticks at 50 Hz), "Sweep
  channel 0", "Never begun" (asleep, every output LOW) and "I2C scan" (0x40 and the All Call 0x70).
- **See-through**: the chip's die with its oscillator ring and sixteen output stages, each lit while its channel sends
  pulses; current on SDA, SCL, VCC, V+ and the first three outputs.
- **Break it**: A0 bridged (the board moves to 0x41 and stops answering), a cold joint on SDA (writes go missing), C2
  left out, VCC shorted to GND.

The bench (Robotics Bench Fig. 08, blush palette): on the pale green mat an **Arduino Nano** left of the board (5V to
VCC, GND, A4 to SDA, A5 to SCL), a **4 x AA holder** behind it with its leads in the V+ terminal, and in front a small
**three-servo desk arm**: an orange acrylic base plate with an upright SG90 turning a turntable (channel 0), a printed
block on the turntable carrying the shoulder SG90 on its side (channel 1), a 70 mm orange link and a gripper SG90 with
two fingers that open sideways (channel 2). The servo leads (brown, red, orange) run from the arm's base to their plugs
and on to the first header block. A 16 mm red foam cube waits on the mat.

What it does (bench):

- **Set up the mat ▾**: the Nano and its four wires, the arm and its leads, the pack.
- **Upload sketch ▾**: Centre the arm (every joint to 90° and the Nano idles: the chip keeps the pulses going), Sweep
  the base (0-180-0 in 6 s), Pick and place (eight poses a second each, every joint eased a degree at a time; the arm
  picks the cube up at 45°, carries it to 135° and puts it down, then the next round carries it back), I2C scan.
- **Model**: each servo turns toward its pulse at 600°/s while V+ holds it up; the pack (6 V, 0.6 Ω) sags with the
  servos' current (10 mA holding, 180 moving, 450 stalled); the cube is caught when the fingers close round it.
- **Break it ▾**: the pack reversed (Q1 blocks it, the servos go limp, the LED stays on, the pack glows), 14500 Li-ion
  cells in the holder (16.8 V: the servos buzz, then burn out grey with smoke, the cells turn blue), the servos on the
  Nano's 5 V (brown-out), no setPWMFreq(50) (a 1 ms frame: the servos twitch), A0 bridged, C2 backwards (it pops), a
  cold SDA joint (the arm stutters), the gripper closed to 0° on the cube (it stalls and buzzes, the arm glows).

State the host reads: `status`, `power`, `mode`, `matStep`, `rails.VCC`, `pwm.hz`, `pwm.us`, `arm.base`,
`arm.shoulder`, `arm.grip`, `arm.vplus`, `arm.ma`, `arm.held`, `arm.states`, `readings.ARM`, `readings.PACK`, `faults`,
`missing`. The lesson is `src/content/robotics/pca9685.ts`.

Look: Colorfigures palette, warm paper, heavy ink silhouette, finer inner lines, hand-drawn wobble, paper grain. Works
on a phone and inside the SimLab host (embedded, bare).
